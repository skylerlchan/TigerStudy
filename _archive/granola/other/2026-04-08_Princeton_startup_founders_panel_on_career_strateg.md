## Summary

### Princeton Alumni Startup Panel

- Panel featuring 4 Princeton alumni in startup roles
- Grant Proctor: Director of Sales at customer support company (2 years, founding salesperson)- Speaker from Cognition: Head of Talent, founding recruiter (August start, 30→250 people)- Speaker from Tenor: VP Business Operations (2.5 years, built out GDA functions)- Legal recruiting founder: Executive search for startups

### Resume & Hiring Insights

- What stands out beyond Princeton credentials:
- Evidence of extraordinary performance among peers- Student athlete status, TA roles, top repo contributions- Ability to navigate chaos and thrive in messy environments
- Social proof trumps polished resumes
- Referrals from high performers carry most weight- Internal recommendations more valuable than cover letters
- Networking strategy:
- List everyone you know, score 1-3 on job potential and relationship strength- Systematically work through conversations- Focus on helping others, not just asking for favors- Never keep score - give more than you expect

### Sales Career Perspectives

- Common misconception: Strategy > Sales
- Reality: Sales involves significant strategic thinking- Direct customer interface provides actionable insights- Managing book of business like running own P&L
- Technical sales evolution:
- First 9-10 reps at Cognition required technical background- 75-80% of Tenor’s code written by AI agents- All executives need sales skills regardless of function
- Sales as “transfer of enthusiasm” - passion must be genuine

### Founder Evaluation Criteria

- Best founders personally involved in recruiting process
- CEO at Cognition still interviews every campus hire at 500+ people- Founder recruitment involvement indicates company priorities
- Red flags: Founders not engaged in talent acquisition
- Talent war critical for first 10-300 employees
- Every hire is company-defining- 70% of CEO time spent on recruiting activities

### AI & Technical Skills

- Non-negotiable: Proficiency with Claude/ChatGPT across all roles
- Coding considerations:
- 90% of code at Cognition written by agents- Engineers still need foundational understanding- Discernment crucial - avoid “accepting everything” without thought
- Balance: Use AI extensively but maintain critical thinking skills

### Startup Culture Commonalities

- Bias for action over perfection
- “Better to solve 8/10 problems than 3 perfectly”- Three-meeting rule: Problems escalated after 3 discussions
- High trust environment due to rigorous hiring standards
- Problem-solving mentality across all functions
- Optimism and fun despite intense work demands

### Work-Life Balance Reality

- Early-stage startups require significant time commitment
- Not enforced but naturally emerges from passion- Work becomes fulfilling when building “generational company”
- Sustainable when entire team shares same drive
- Burnout risk if you’re only one pushing hard
- Trade-off: Massive rewards if successful, but requires total dedication

### Early Employee Success Factors

- Feedback receptiveness and quick iteration
- Bias for action: “See hole in ground, fill it yourself”
- Come with solutions, not just problems
- Collaboration over individual achievement (especially in sales)
- Document work and maintain updated resume
- Proactive career conversations: “What needs to be true for promotion in 3-6 months?”

### Company Differentiation & Moats

- Technology moats disappear quickly in current environment
- Only sustainable advantage: “Being badass longer than everyone else”
- Continuous innovation and superior execution
- Commercial-minded leadership as competitive advantage
