HW4 Q5: Variance of the Number of Love Triangles

ORF 309 — A step-by-step visual walkthrough

1

Setup & Notation

n people on a dating site. Each pair is independently "hooked up" with probability p.

A love triangle = 3 people who are ALL mutually hooked up (all 3 edges present).

Define an indicator variable for each triple {i, j, k}:

I{u,v,w} = 1 if all 3 edges are present,   0 otherwise

Then the total number of love triangles N is:

N = Σ{u,v,w} I{u,v,w}

From HW3, you already know:

E[N] = C(n,3) · p3

because P(I{u,v,w} = 1) = p3 (3 independent edges each with prob p).

2

Expanding N² to Get Var(N)

We use Var(N) = E[N²] − (E[N])². We already have E[N], so we need E[N²].

Squaring the sum gives a double sum over all pairs of triples:

N² = (Σ{u,v,w} I{u,v,w})(Σ{u',v',w'} I{u',v',w'}) = Σ{u,v,w} Σ{u',v',w'} I{u,v,w} · I{u',v',w'}

Taking expectation:

E[N²] = Σ{u,v,w} Σ{u',v',w'} E[I{u,v,w} · I{u',v',w'}]

So to compute E[N²], we need to evaluate E[I · I'] for every pair of triples. This depends on how many people the two triples share.

Key question: When does E[I{u,v,w} · I{u',v',w'}] ≠ E[I] · E[I'] = p&sup6;? Only when the two triples share an edge (i.e., share at least 2 people). If they share 0 or 1 people, their edges are all independent, so the product factors: E[I · I'] = p&sup6;.
3

How Can Two Triples Overlap?

Two triples of people can share 0, 1, 2, or 3 people. Let's see which cases matter:

0 shared people

6 distinct edges, all independent

E[I1I2] = p6 = E[I1]E[I2]
Cov = 0 → doesn't matter
# of pairs: irrelevant
1 shared person

5 people, but still 6 distinct edges

E[I1I2] = p6 = E[I1]E[I2]
Cov = 0 → doesn't matter
# of pairs: irrelevant
2 shared people (1 shared edge)

4 people, 5 distinct edges (1 shared!)

E[I1I2] = p5 ≠ p6
Cov = p5 − p6 = p5(1−p) ≠ 0
# of pairs: C(n,2) · C(n−2, 2)
Pick the 2 shared people: C(n,2)
Pick 2 others (one per triple): C(n−2, 2)
3 shared (same triple)

Same triple — this is the diagonal term

E[I²] = E[I] = p3
Contributes to diagonal
# of pairs: C(n, 3)
One for each triple
4

Why Only the "2 Shared" Case Creates Dependence

Two indicators I{u,v,w} and I{u',v',w'} are independent when they depend on completely separate coin flips.

  • 0 shared people: 6 people, 6 distinct edges → 6 independent coins → E[I1I2] = p3 · p3 = p6
  • 1 shared person: 5 people, but still 6 distinct edges (no edge is shared!) → still independent → p6
  • 2 shared people: 4 people, but only 5 distinct edges (the shared pair's edge is used by both triangles). The two indicators are not independent because if the shared edge is "off," both triangles fail → E[I1I2] = p5
The intuition: Sharing a person doesn't create dependence — sharing an edge does. Two people must be shared before any edge is shared, because edges connect pairs.
5

Counting the Pairs

Diagonal: how many triples?

C(n, 3) triples total

Each contributes Var(I) = p3(1 − p3) to the diagonal.

Off-diagonal: how many unordered pairs of triples share exactly 2 people?

Count them by construction:

  1. Choose the 2 shared people: C(n, 2) ways
  2. Choose 2 different non-shared people (one for each triple): C(n−2, 2) ways
Number of pairs sharing exactly 2 people = C(n, 2) · C(n−2, 2)

Each contributes Cov = p5(1 − p) to the off-diagonal (multiplied by 2 for the symmetric sum).

6

Putting It All Together

Var(N) = Σi Var(Ii) + 2 Σi < j, share 2 people Cov(Ii, Ij)
Var(N) = C(n,3) · p3(1 − p3)  +  2 · C(n,2) · C(n−2,2) · p5(1 − p)

diagonal (each triple's own variance)  +  off-diagonal (pairs of triples sharing an edge)

✓

Roadmap for Writing Your Solution

  1. Define N = Σ{u,v,w} I{u,v,w} and write Var(N) = Σ Var(I) + 2Σ Cov(Ii, Ij)
  2. Compute Var(I{u,v,w}) = E[I2] − (E[I])2 = p3 − p6
  3. Classify pairs of distinct triples by overlap (0, 1, or 2 shared people)
  4. Show Cov = 0 for 0 or 1 shared people (independence of edges)
  5. Show E[I1I2] = p5 for 2 shared people → Cov = p5 − p6
  6. Count pairs sharing exactly 2 people: C(n,2) · C(n−2, 2)
  7. Combine into the final formula
