# ECO 310 -- Slide Weight Methodology

Complete problem-by-problem mapping of every midterm question to the specific slide deck that teaches the required material.

---

## Methodology

1. Extracted every question from all 4 past midterms (Spring 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025) with point values
2. Identified the core concept tested in each question
3. Matched each concept to the specific lecture slide deck where it is taught
4. Summed points per slide deck per exam to compute weight percentages

**Slide deck abbreviations used below:**
- **L1** = Lecture 1 (Preferences and Choice folder: `intermediate_lec1_math_26.pdf`) -- Preference relations, completeness, transitivity, rationality definitions
- **L2** = Lecture 2 (Preferences and Choice folder: `ec 310 lec2 spring25.pdf`) -- Indifference curves, MRS intro, revealed preference, IIA, Sen's alpha/beta, WARP
- **L3&4** = Lectures 3&4 (Preferences and Choice folder: `ec 310 lec3 and 4 spring26.pdf`) -- Convexity, utility function types (Cobb-Douglas, perfect substitutes/complements, quasilinear, CES), MRS formulas
- **L5&6** = Lectures 5&6 (Preferences and Choice folder: `ec 310 lec5 and 6 spring 26.pdf`) -- Budget constraint, tangency condition, Lagrangian, Marshallian demand, Hicksian demand, expenditure function, corner solutions, sub/income effects, compensating variation, indirect utility, duality
- **L7&8** = Lectures 7&8 (Choice Under Uncertainty folder: `intermediate_lec7 and 8 math26.pdf`) -- Lotteries, EU theorem, vNM axioms, risk aversion = concavity, CE, risk premium, Arrow-Pratt, CARA/CRRA/DARA, Jensen's inequality, stochastic dominance, insurance
- **L9** = Lecture 9 (Choice Under Uncertainty folder: `intermediate_lec9 spring 26.pdf`) -- EU applications continued, portfolio/asset allocation, Allais paradox, insurance extensions, mean-preserving spreads (applied)
- **L10b** = Lecture 10b (Markets folder: `ec310 lecture10b spring26.pdf`) -- Edgeworth box, Pareto efficiency, competitive equilibrium, welfare theorems, production

## Notes on Judgment Calls

Some problems span material from multiple lectures. Here's how I resolved ambiguities:

1. **Cobb-Douglas magic rule MC questions** (e.g., 2023 MC2, 2024 MC3, 2025 MC1): Assigned to **L5&6**. The utility function type is introduced in L3&4, but the spending share formula is the optimization result derived in L5&6. The question tests whether you can apply the optimization shortcut, not whether you recognize the utility type.

2. **CES limit behavior** (2025 SA1b): Assigned to **L3&4**. This tests understanding of how CES nests perfect complements, Cobb-Douglas, and perfect substitutes as special cases -- which is conceptual knowledge about utility function families, the core content of L3&4.

3. **CES Marshallian demand** (2025 SA1a) and **CES expenditure function** (2025 SA1c): Assigned to **L5&6**. These test the MRS = price ratio derivation technique and expenditure minimization, which are L5&6 methods, even though the CES form is introduced earlier.

4. **DARA / log utility gamble acceptance** (2025 SA2b): Assigned to **L7&8**. DARA is defined in L7&8 (Arrow-Pratt section), and the problem is about computing when EU(accept) > EU(reject), which is core EU optimization from L7&8. L9 also discusses DARA but as review/application.

5. **Mean-preserving spread MC questions** (2023 MC6, 2024 MC8): Assigned to **L9**. While stochastic dominance is introduced in L7&8, the applied "which gamble would a risk-averse person prefer" framing is the application-oriented material of L9.

6. **Convexity sub-questions embedded in demand problems** (e.g., 2023 Q3a, 2024 Q3b): Assigned to **L3&4** since convexity verification (declining MRS) is the specific skill tested, even though the question appears within a larger demand problem.

7. **Perfect complements sub/income effect** (2023 MC4, 2024 MC5): Assigned to **L5&6**. The decomposition technique (Slutsky equation, compensated bundle) is L5&6 content. The fact that the utility type is perfect complements (L3&4) is secondary.

8. **2025 SA3 (correlated assets, portfolio FOC)**: Assigned entirely to **L7&8**. While L9 covers portfolio allocation, the specific skills tested here -- writing EU with multiple states, taking FOC, interpreting marginal utility conditions -- are all from the L7&8 theoretical framework. L9 covers the simpler risk-free + risky asset case.

The earlier analysis grouped L7&8 and L9 together. With this granular mapping, the data shows **L7&8 alone is the highest-weight deck** at 43.1%, slightly above L5&6 at 35.8%.
