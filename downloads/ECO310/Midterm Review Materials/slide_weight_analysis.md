## Detailed Breakdown by Topic Area

### 1. Consumer Optimization -- Lec 5&6 (~42% of exam)

This is the single most important slide deck. It covers nearly half the exam by points.

**What gets tested from this deck:**

| Topic | Frequency | Typical Format | Points |
|-------|-----------|---------------|--------|
| Marshallian demand derivation | 4/4 exams | Long-answer (10-12 pts) | High |
| Cobb-Douglas magic rule | 4/4 exams | MC (4 pts) | Medium |
| Perfect complements demand | 4/4 exams | MC or long-answer | Medium-High |
| Expenditure function / Hicksian demand | 3/4 exams | Long-answer (10 pts) | High |
| Substitution + income effect | 3/4 exams | MC (4 pts) | Medium |
| Corner solutions / non-negativity | 3/4 exams | MC or embedded in long-answer | Medium |
| Perfect substitutes (bang-per-buck) | 3/4 exams | MC (4 pts) | Medium |
| Compensating variation | 2/4 exams | Long-answer | Medium |
| CES utility | 1/4 but tested exhaustively in 2025 (32 pts) | Long-answer | High when it appears |
| Labor-leisure / endowment budget | 2/4 exams | Long-answer | Medium |

**Study priority within this deck:**
1. Be able to derive Marshallian demand for Cobb-Douglas, perfect complements, perfect substitutes, quasilinear, and CES
2. Know the Cobb-Douglas spending share rule cold (normalize exponents, then fraction = exponent/sum)
3. Practice expenditure minimization / Hicksian demand separately from Marshallian -- confusing these was a major grading deduction in 2024
4. Understand when corner solutions arise and how to check non-negativity

### 2. Choice Under Uncertainty -- Lec 7&8 (~30% of exam)

The theoretical backbone of the uncertainty section.

**What gets tested from this deck:**

| Topic | Frequency | Typical Format | Points |
|-------|-----------|---------------|--------|
| Setting up EU expression | 4/4 exams | Long-answer (8-12 pts) | High |
| FOC optimization of EU | 4/4 exams | Long-answer (9-12 pts) | High |
| Risk aversion = concavity | 4/4 exams | MC or proof | Medium-High |
| Certainty equivalent computation | 3/4 exams | Long-answer (10 pts) | High |
| Risk premium | 3/4 exams | Embedded in CE problems | Medium |
| Jensen's inequality / EU proofs | 2/4 exams | Proof (10-12 pts) | Medium |
| CRRA utility | 2/4 exams | Long-answer | Medium |
| Comparing risk aversion across agents | 1/4 exams | Proof (12 pts) | Medium |

**Study priority within this deck:**
1. Practice writing EU expressions from word problems -- identify states, probabilities, and wealth in each state
2. Practice taking FOC of EU w.r.t. choice variable and solving
3. Know the definition: risk averse iff u(E[W]) >= EU iff u is concave
4. Be able to compute CE from u(CE) = EU and risk premium = E[W] - CE

### 3. Choice Under Uncertainty -- Lec 9 (~16% of exam)

Applications and extensions of uncertainty theory.

**What gets tested from this deck:**

| Topic | Frequency | Typical Format | Points |
|-------|-----------|---------------|--------|
| Asset allocation (risky + risk-free) | 3/4 exams | MC or long-answer | Medium-High |
| Mean-preserving spread / contraction | 3/4 exams | MC (4 pts) | Medium |
| Consequentialism / Allais-type paradox | 2/4 exams (same question reused) | MC (4 pts) | Low-Medium |
| DARA / log utility thresholds | 2/4 exams | Long-answer (11 pts) | Medium |
| Portfolio std dev / expected return | 1/4 exams | MC (4 pts) | Low |

**Study priority within this deck:**
1. Portfolio problems: know that portfolio std dev = alpha * asset std dev, and E[return] = alpha * risky + (1-alpha) * risk-free
2. Mean-preserving spread: same EV but less spread is preferred by all risk-averse agents
3. DARA with log utility: be able to find the wealth threshold for accepting/rejecting a gamble

---

### 4. Preferences Theory -- Lec 2, 3&4 (~9% of exam combined)

Foundational theory that shows up mostly in MC questions. Declining in weight over time.

**What gets tested from these decks:**

| Topic | Frequency | Source Deck | Typical Format |
|-------|-----------|------------|---------------|
| WARP / Revealed Preference | 3/4 exams | Lec 2 | MC or short-answer (4-8 pts) |
| Rationality (completeness + transitivity) | 3/4 exams | Lec 1, 3&4 | MC (4 pts) |
| Convexity of preferences | 3/4 exams | Lec 3&4 | Embedded in long-answer (6-8 pts) |
| IIA | 2/4 exams | Lec 2 | Proof or MC |

**Study priority:**
1. WARP: know the definition, be able to check if two bundles/price observations violate WARP by computing costs at each price vector
2. Completeness vs. transitivity: be able to identify which fails in a given example (they test tricky cases where one holds but not the other)
3. Convexity: know that declining MRS = convex preferences, and how to verify

---

### 5. Lecture 1 (~3% of exam)

Just definitions of preference relations, completeness, transitivity. These are tested via MC questions that ask "is this preference rational?" Knowing the content from Lec 2 and 3&4 implicitly covers Lec 1.

---

### 6. Markets -- Lec 10b (0% historically)

Edgeworth box, Pareto efficiency, competitive equilibrium, welfare theorems, production economy. **Never tested on any of the 4 past midterms.** However, this material is on the current syllabus, so it could appear. If you're short on time, this is the lowest priority, but don't skip it entirely if the professor has indicated it's fair game.

## Key Trend: Uncertainty Weight is Growing

Across the 4 exams, the combined uncertainty weight (Lec 7&8 + Lec 9) has increased from 44% (2022) to 53% (2025), while preference theory has dropped from 22% to 4%. Consumer optimization has remained stable at ~40-50%. Plan accordingly.
