Hi all, hope you've had a great break so far. As you hopefully can see, I uploaded exam scans to Gradescope, so you should be able to see your exam and more detailed feedback there. The raw mean was 69.57/89, or about 78%. I adjusted this a tiny bit on Canvas, so there I reduced the total to 85 (i.e. dropped a 4-point question) and posted your percentage grade with this adjustment. This puts the mean at 82%, which is what I typically aim for. 

Here are intended [solutions.](https://princeton.instructure.com/courses/20994/files/4509269?verifier=31iWsU62Gljht4RTNlxjNxblNspfPTgWBOvVcFO6&wrap=1) 

Many students did extremely well on this, but it was a bit troubling how many struggled on short answer (b)-(d) which were intended to be very straightforward. I suspect some were looking for a trick question that wasn't there, and some perhaps struggled under pressure. In any case, we are done with the "bad" part of the semester, which is probably most boring for everyone and most difficult for most, and moving on to more interesting topics :). 
I graded somewhat quickly so if you find an error, it is easiest if you request a regrade directly on Gradescope. 

EDIT: I haven't yet determined or posted letter grades, but typically the average student is an A-. I will give more info about this by Monday and may post midterm letter grades. Most students do better on the final, and most problem set grades are very high, so at the end of the semester, the A cutoff usually ranges from 88-91 and A- from 85-88. But on this particular exam, both cutoffs would start lower than this. If you are fairly close to the mean you can expect you are currently on track for an A-. My B+ range is "good, just missed A-", and then B range is wider to reflect "missed many things but there is clearly some understanding". There would have been a few B-'s on this exam, nobody C.
