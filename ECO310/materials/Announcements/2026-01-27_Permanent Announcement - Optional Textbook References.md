# Permanent Announcement: Optional Textbook References

**Posted:** 2026-01-27

**Author:** Andrea Wilson


Hi all, 3 students have already requested textbook suggestions, so I'll post them here. Note that everything you need to know is in the lecture slides, so these are optional. I will be teaching a course somewhere in between Varian's "Intermediate Microeconomics: With Calculus" (you can rent this from Amazon) and a book by Ariel Rubinstein and Martin Osborne, "[Models in Economic Theory](https://arielrubinstein.org/gt/arielDocs/)", which you can download for free from Rubinstein's website. A couple post-midterm topics are unfortunately not yet in any standard textbook. 

**Weeks 1-2**: I'm pretty close here to the Osborne-Rubinstein book, chapters 1-2 and 4-5. (But note that in chapter 2, they assume singleton choice sets; with this, IIA is equivalent to rationalizability, thus they do not discuss Sen's beta axiom as we will in Lecture 2). Varian doesn't have much of our week 1 material but for week 2, you can look at  Chapters 3-5, and Chapter 7 up to p. 126

**Lecture 5:** in Varian, a bit more Ch 5, then Ch 6 up to p.106, then Ch 8 up to p.144, and also p.157.

**Lectures 6-8: **In Varian, chapters 12-13; in Osborne-Rubinstein, chapter 3. We will be a bit more theoretical than Varian and a bit less theoretical than Osborne-Rubinstein, and with some applications not discussed in either, but both should be helpful references. 

**Lectures 9-10: **Varian pages 258-261, 273-275, 277-279, and most of chapter 16

POST MIDTERM

**Lectures 11-12: **closest to Varian chapters 21-23, and we'll do some stuff from Varian's monopoly chapter, but in much less depth (only 1/3 to 1/2 of a class on monopoly for us). 

**Lectures 13-14: **Exchange economies and welfare theorems are covered in both Varian chapter 32 and Osborne-Rubinstein Ch 10. Double auctions and rational expectations aren't really covered in either of these textbooks, nor any others that I know. 

**Lectures 15-16: ** not in Varian, but quite a bit of this is in Osborne-Rubinstein Chapter 18

**Lectures 17-22** (game theory): our treatment will be much closer to Osborne-Rubinstein than to Varian. I recommend their Chapters 15-16.
