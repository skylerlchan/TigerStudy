## Summary

### Lexicographic Preferences

- Not continuous preferences - cannot maintain strict preference with small adjustments
- Example: (0,1) vs (0,0) - adding small amount of good 1 flips preference- No utility representation possible
- Still rational (complete and transitive) but lack continuity
- Convexity demonstration
- If x ≻ y, then average bundle also ≻ y- Two cases: different good 1 quantities or same good 1, different good 2

### Revealed Preference (WARP)

- Violation occurs when choice changes and expensive option chosen both times
- Example: Buy A for $100 when B costs $90, later buy B for $80 when A costs $70
- First choice reveals A ≻ B (paid extra $10)- Second choice reveals B ≻ A (contradiction)
- No violation when choosing cheaper option - multiple explanations possible

### Marshallian Demand

- Standard utility maximization with budget constraint
- Two-good approach: equate marginal utility ratios to price ratios
- Special cases:
- Perfect complements: equate quantities in brackets, not marginal utilities- Perfect substitutes: compare bang-for-buck ratios
- If MU₁/P₁ > MU₂/P₂: buy only good 1- If MU₁/P₁ < MU₂/P₂: buy only good 2- If equal: any combination optimal

### Hicksian (Compensated) Demand

- Minimize expenditure subject to utility constraint
- Same allocation condition as Marshallian
- Replace budget constraint with utility constraint: U(x₁,x₂) = u*
- No income parameter in solution
- Example with perfect complements U = min{x₁,x₂}:
- Optimal allocation: x₁ = x₂- Hicksian demands: x₁ᶜ = x₂ᶜ = u*- Expenditure function: E(p₁,p₂,u*) = (p₁ + p₂)u*

### Income and Substitution Effects

- Substitution effect: x^c - x^o (always negative for own price increase)
- Income effect: x^f - x^c
- Method:
- Calculate initial bundle x^o at original prices/income- Calculate final bundle x^f at new prices/original income- Calculate compensated bundle x^c at new prices/original utility
- Compensating variation (CV) typically less than cost increase of original bundle
- Exception: when no substitution possible (perfect complements at extreme ratios)

### Expected Utility Theory

- Axioms: completeness, transitivity, continuity, independence
- EU = Σ pᵢU(xᵢ) - probability-weighted average of outcome utilities
- Inconsistency test: write out EU equations, show contradiction
- Independence axiom: focus on differences when comparing lotteries

### Risk Preferences

- Risk averse: concave utility, EU from gamble < U(expected wealth)
- Risk loving: convex utility, EU from gamble > U(expected wealth)
- Certainty equivalent: guaranteed amount equal in value to risky gamble
- Risk premium: expected wealth - certainty equivalent

### bonus

- Independence axiom violations through lottery comparisons
- Show contradictions in expected utility maximization
- Some type of independence contradiction
