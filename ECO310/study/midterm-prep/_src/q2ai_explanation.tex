\section*{The Question}

\textbf{Show that someone prefers accepting (rather than rejecting) a risky bet if and only if their certainty equivalent for the gamble exceeds their initial wealth.}

\section*{Key Definitions}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!5!white,colframe=blue!75!black,title=Important Terms]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{$w_0$}: Initial wealth (what you have now before deciding on the bet)
    \item \textbf{$u(w_0)$}: Utility from keeping your initial wealth (rejecting the bet)
    \item \textbf{EU}: Expected utility from taking the risky bet
    \item \textbf{CE}: Certainty equivalent -- the guaranteed amount that makes you indifferent to the gamble
    \item \textbf{Key relationship}: $u(CE) = EU$ (by definition of CE)
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\section*{What We Need to Prove}

We need to prove a \textbf{bi-directional} relationship (if and only if):

\begin{center}
\fbox{Person prefers accepting the bet $\Leftrightarrow$ $CE > w_0$}
\end{center}

This requires proving \textbf{two directions}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Forward direction ($\Rightarrow$)}: If they prefer accepting the bet, then $CE > w_0$
    \item \textbf{Backward direction ($\Leftarrow$)}: If $CE > w_0$, then they prefer accepting the bet
\end{enumerate}

\newpage

\section*{Step-by-Step Proof}

\subsection*{Direction 1: If prefer accepting bet $\Rightarrow$ $CE > w_0$}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=\textbf{Step \arabic*:}]
    \item \textbf{Start with the assumption}

    Assume the person prefers accepting the bet over rejecting it.

    \vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{What does this mean in terms of utility?}

    Preferring the bet means:
    \begin{equation}
    EU > u(w_0)
    \end{equation}

    The expected utility from the risky bet exceeds the utility from keeping initial wealth.

    \vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Use the definition of certainty equivalent}

    By definition, the certainty equivalent satisfies:
    \begin{equation}
    u(CE) = EU
    \end{equation}

    \vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Substitute}

    From Steps 2 and 3:
    \begin{align}
    EU &> u(w_0) \\
    u(CE) &> u(w_0) \quad \text{(since } u(CE) = EU\text{)}
    \end{align}

    \vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Apply monotonicity of utility}

    Since utility is increasing in wealth (more money = higher utility), we know:
    \begin{equation}
    u(CE) > u(w_0) \implies CE > w_0
    \end{equation}

    \vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Conclusion}

    Therefore, if someone prefers accepting the bet, then $CE > w_0$. \qed
\end{enumerate}

\newpage

\subsection*{Direction 2: If $CE > w_0$ $\Rightarrow$ prefer accepting bet}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=\textbf{Step \arabic*:}]
    \item \textbf{Start with the assumption}

    Assume $CE > w_0$.

    \vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Apply monotonicity of utility}

    Since utility is increasing in wealth:
    \begin{equation}
    CE > w_0 \implies u(CE) > u(w_0)
    \end{equation}

    More wealth gives higher utility.

    \vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Use the definition of certainty equivalent}

    By definition:
    \begin{equation}
    u(CE) = EU
    \end{equation}

    \vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Substitute}

    From Steps 2 and 3:
    \begin{align}
    u(CE) &> u(w_0) \\
    EU &> u(w_0) \quad \text{(since } EU = u(CE)\text{)}
    \end{align}

    \vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Interpret the inequality}

    $EU > u(w_0)$ means the expected utility from the risky bet exceeds the utility from keeping initial wealth.

    \vspace{0.3cm}

    \item \textbf{Conclusion}

    Therefore, the person prefers accepting the bet over rejecting it. \qed
\end{enumerate}

\newpage

\section*{Summary}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=green!5!white,colframe=green!75!black,title=The Complete Proof]
We've proven both directions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Forward}: Prefer bet $\Rightarrow$ $EU > u(w_0) \Rightarrow u(CE) > u(w_0) \Rightarrow CE > w_0$

    \item \textbf{Backward}: $CE > w_0 \Rightarrow u(CE) > u(w_0) \Rightarrow EU > u(w_0) \Rightarrow$ Prefer bet
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Key insight}: The certainty equivalent acts as a threshold. If the ``sure thing'' value (CE) that makes you indifferent to the gamble exceeds what you currently have ($w_0$), then the gamble must be attractive enough to accept.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section*{Intuition}

Think of it this way:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item The CE is the guaranteed amount that makes you say ``I'm indifferent between having this for sure vs. taking the gamble.''

    \item If $CE > w_0$, it means you'd need to be given \textit{more} than your current wealth to be indifferent to the gamble.

    \item But if you don't take the bet, you only get $w_0$ (your current wealth).

    \item Since the bet is ``worth'' CE to you (in terms of guaranteed value), and $CE > w_0$, the bet is better than keeping $w_0$.

    \item Therefore: $CE > w_0$ means the bet is attractive!
\end{itemize}
