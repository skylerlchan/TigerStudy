Q2: Proofs and General Concepts (20 Points)
a. (12) Assume that Melsi and Mavi (my dogs) are equally wealthy. Both are risk-averse, but
Mavi is more risk-averse.
Prove through the following steps that any time Mavi prefers
accepting a risky bet (to rejecting it), so does Melsi:
i. (6) Show that someone prefers accepting (rather than rejecting) a risky bet if and only
if their certainty equivalent for the gamble exceeds their initial wealth.
Answer: accepting the bet implies that its EU, which equals u(CE) by deﬁnition, exceeds
u(w0); since utility increases with wealth, u(CE) > u(w0) ⇒CE > w0. And conversely
if CE > w0, then u(CE) > u(w0) since u is increasing, while EU = u(CE) by deﬁnition
of the certainty equivalent; so EU > u(w0), and so this person prefers to take the bet
since it yields a higher utility EU than sticking with initial wealth.
ii. (6) If Mavi is more risk-averse than Melsi, then from Lecture 10, her risk premium is
higher for every gamble. Prove that this implies that for a particular gamble, Melsi’s
certainty equivalent is higher than Mavi’s.
Answer: The risk premium π is deﬁned by EU = u(E[W]−π), i.e. the amount of wealth
π that the decision-maker would sacriﬁce to get a sure thing. We also have EU = u(CE),
and so comparing gives E[W] −CE = π. So for any gamble, Mavi’s π is higher than
Melsi’s; since E[W] is the same (they are facing the same gamble), it follows that Mavi’s
CE is lower than Melsi’s.
Since they are initially equally wealthy, this means by (a) that whenever Mavi’s CE
exceeds initial wealth w0 and so she accepts the bet by (a), so does Melsi’s.
