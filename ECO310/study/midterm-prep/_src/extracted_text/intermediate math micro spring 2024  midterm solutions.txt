1. Jake cares about three attributes of cars: price, quality, and color. When comparing two cars,
he prefers whichever is better in two attributes. Jake is:
(a) Rational
(b) Irrational because these preferences are incomplete
(c) Irrational because these preferences are intransitive
(d) Two of these are correct
Answer: C. They are complete—for any two cars, one of them has to be best in two attributes,
so this one is preferred. But they are intransitive (there was a voting example like this in
precept 1). For example, suppose comparisons are that in terms of price, car 1 beats car 2
beats car 3; in terms of quality, car 2 beats car 3 beats car 1; and in terms of color, car 3
beats car 1 beats car 2. Then car 1 is preferred to car 2 (better for price and color), car 2 is
preferred to car 3 (better in terms of price and quality), but car 1 is NOT preferred to car
3, as would be required by transitivity, since car 3 beats car 1 in terms of both quality and
color. if car 1 beats car 2 because of price a; and this leaves at most one attribute where the

8. Which of the following would a risk-averse consumer deﬁnitely prefer to a 50-50 $0 or $200
gamble? Pick ALL that apply.
(a) $90 lump-sum
(b) $110 lump-sum
(c) A 50-50 $50 or $150 gamble

Answer: B,C,D are all correct. The gamble has expected wealth $100, so B is clearly
better (more money with less risk) but A may not be (less risk is good, less money is
bad). C and D are also better as they are keeping expected wealth at $100, but moving
the outcomes closer together by equal amounts is reducing the risk, which a risk-averse
consumer likes.
9. On Monday, Homer Simpson buys a bundle of food and drink that costs $100. On Tuesday,
he buys a diﬀerent bundle, for $80, which would have cost $100 on Monday. In which of the
following situations does Homer violate WARP?
(a) If the Monday bundle would cost more than $80 at Tuesday’s prices.
(b) If the Monday bundle would cost less than $80 at Tuesday’s prices.
(c) If the Monday bundle would cost less than $100 at Tuesday’s prices
(d) Homer does not violate WARP
(e) Not enough information given.
Answer: B. On Monday, the bundle purchased costs $100, and the Tues bundle would
also have cost $100; this reveals that the Monday bundle is weakly preferred. So Homer
violates WARP if his Tues choice suggests a strict preference for the Tues bundle, namely
if the Monday bundle would cost less than $80 at Tuesday’s prices.
