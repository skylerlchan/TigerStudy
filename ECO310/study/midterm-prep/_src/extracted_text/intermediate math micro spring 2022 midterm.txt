Q1: Proofs and General Concepts
a. Sally has preferences over X = R2 represented by u(x1, x2) = max{x1, x2}. Are her prefer-
ences (i) rational? (ii) convex?
b. Now Sally tells you that she has a utility function u : X →R, and, from any menu A ⊆X,
chooses the option in A with the lowest utility (C(A) = arg minx∈A u(x)). Do her choices
satisfy IIA? (proof or counterexample)
c. Using only the assumption that utility of expected wealth beats expected utility, prove that
a gamble with 50-50 odds of winning or losing $100 is preferred to a gamble with 50-50 odds
of winning or losing $200.

Q2: Demand
Homer Simpson’s preferences over beer (B) and leisure (L) are given by
u(B, L) = min{B, L}
He spends 8h per day passed out, but can work as many of the remaining 16 hours as he wishes,
at a ﬂat wage w/hour. Marge also gives him an allowance of N per day. He buys cheap beer, with
price pB = 1.
a. Find his optimal labor supply, ℓ(w). (i.e., how many hours will he optimally work at wage
w?)
b. Calculate his compensating variation if the price of beer doubles.
c. How much allowance (N) does Homer need at beer price pB and wage rate w to get utility
u∗? (Assume u∗≤16)
