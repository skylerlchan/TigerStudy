## MNEMONICS

### The 5 Contributions of Early Jazz: **"B.C.L.B.C."** (Big Cats Love Big Catches)

1. **B**irth of Jazz (New Orleans, ~1900, fusion of African/blues/ragtime/European)
2. **C**ollective Improvisation (heterophony)
3. **L**ouis Armstrong's Revolution (ensemble -> soloist; scat singing)
4. **B**lues Form Codification (12-bar AAB)
5. **C**ommercial Viability (first recordings)

### Era Characteristics: **"R.E.I.B.R.S."** (Remember Every Item Before Real Study)

- **R**hythmic feel (swing, beats 2 and 4)
- **E**nsemble style (trumpet, clarinet, trombone = marching band reduction)
- **I**mprovisation (collective / heterophony)
- **B**lues influence (pentatonic + tritone, blue notes b3 b5 b7, call and response)
- **R**ecording tech (~3 min per side)
- **S**ound (instruments mimic vocals)

### Blue Notes: **"3-5-7"** -- all flat, all odd numbers

- Flat 3, Flat 5, Flat 7

### Blues Scale Formula

- Pentatonic + Tritone (the "Devil's Interval")
- Think: **"Penta + Devil = Blues"**

### 12-Bar Blues: **"Say it, Say it again, Resolve it"**

- A (say it) - A (say it again) - B (resolve it)
- 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 bars
- **NOT** AABA (that's the 32-bar pop song form -- different thing entirely)

### Key Concepts: **"C.H.B.B.C.S.C.B."** -- too many? Just remember the groupings:

**The Music Stuff:** Heterophony, Blues Structure (AAB), Blues Scale (penta + tritone), Call and Response, Swing Rhythm

**The History Stuff:** Congo Square (enslaved people, preserved African traditions), Creole (mixed-race, classically trained, barred from white orchestras)

**The Quote:** Baraka -- "Blues could not exist if the African captives had not become American captives"

### Artist Memory - Chronological Order: **"J.H.O.B.M.A."** (Jazz History Opens Before Many Audiences)

| # | Artist | Nickname/Title | Key Song | Year | One-Liner |
|---|--------|---------------|----------|------|-----------|
| 1 | Scott **J**oplin | "King of Ragtime" | Maple Leaf Rag | 1916 roll (1899 sheet) | Piano roll, NO improv, syncopation |
| 2 | W.C. **H**andy | "Father of the Blues" | St. Louis Blues | 1922 | Formally trained, tango/"Spanish tinge" |
| 3 | **O**DJB | First jazz recording | Dixie Jass Band One-Step | 1917 | All-white, rehearsed, Keppard refused |
| 4 | **B**essie Smith | "Empress of the Blues" | Backwater Blues | 1927 | Dual call-and-response, James P. Johnson |
| 5 | Jelly Roll **M**orton | Creole, claimed he invented jazz | Dead Man Blues | 1926 | Red Hot Peppers, spoken intro, funeral march |
| 6 | Louis **A**rmstrong | "First Great Soloist" | West End Blues | 1928 | Trumpet cadenza, vibrato at END, scat (1926) |

### Remembering Freddie Keppard

- **K**eppard = **K**ept his music secret (refused to record, feared copying)
- ODJB recorded first instead

### Armstrong's Innovations - **"S.V.S."** (Soloist, Vibrato, Scat)

1. **S**oloist's art form (shifted jazz from ensemble)
2. **V**ibrato at END of notes only
3. **S**cat singing (1926, "Heebie Jeebies")

### Baraka Quote - Break It Down

> "Blues could not exist if the **African** captives had not become **American** captives"

Key word pair: **African -> American**. Blues is the product of that forced transformation. Without enslavement in America specifically, no blues. The quote is about cultural fusion through captivity.

### Section C

19. Congo Square was a public space in 19th-century New Orleans where enslaved people congregated to make music and dance. It preserved African musical traditions (polyrhythm, call and response, communal music-making) that predated jazz and directly fed into its development.

20. Before Armstrong, jazz was collective ensemble music -- everyone improvised together (heterophony). Armstrong shifted jazz to feature a single virtuoso soloist improvising over accompaniment, making individual expression the center of the music. This transformed the entire art form.

21. Any three of: collective improvisation (multiple instruments improvising simultaneously), swing rhythm (vs. ragtime's stiff feel), trumpet/clarinet/trombone ensemble, improvisation (vs. ragtime's composed nature), blues influence (blue notes, call and response), instruments mimicking vocal techniques.

22. Freddie Keppard, a Black cornet player, was offered the first chance to record jazz but refused because he feared people would copy his style. The ODJB, an all-white band, then made the first commercial jazz recording in 1917 and gained fame/profit from a genre created by Black musicians in New Orleans.
