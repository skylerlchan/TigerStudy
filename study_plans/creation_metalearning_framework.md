# The Creation Meta-Learning Framework

A 7-day cyclical system for mastering any subject, rooted in the theological structure of Genesis 1 and biblical wisdom principles.

---

## How to Use This

This is a **repeating 7-day cycle** you apply to any new subject, module, or skill. Each "day" maps to a phase of the creation narrative and a corresponding cognitive operation. One cycle takes one week. Complex subjects take multiple cycles, each going deeper.

---

## Day 1 -- Separate Light from Darkness

**Genesis principle:** Before anything else, God distinguished light from darkness. The first act of creation is *separation* and *clarity*.

**Learning operation: Epistemic triage**

- Survey the entire scope of what you need to learn (syllabus, textbook TOC, skill tree)
- Explicitly separate what you **already know** (light) from what you **don't know** (darkness)
- Write two lists: "I know" and "I don't know"
- Identify which unknowns are **most critical** -- these are your targets for the cycle

**Theological anchor:** Proverbs 4:7 -- "The beginning of wisdom is this: get wisdom. Though it cost all you have, get understanding." The first move is always honest assessment. You cannot learn what you don't know you don't know.

**Output:** A single page with your known/unknown map and your priority targets for this cycle.

---

## Day 2 -- Separate the Waters: Create Structure

**Genesis principle:** God created the *firmament* -- a structural separation between upper and lower waters. This is the act of creating **categories and containers** before filling them.

**Learning operation: Build the scaffold**

- Take your target unknowns from Day 1 and organize them into **3-5 major categories**
- For each category, identify the **governing question** it answers (e.g., "How does X work?" / "Why does Y happen?" / "When does Z apply?")
- Create a skeleton outline, mind map, or table with empty cells
- Do NOT try to fill in details yet -- only build the architecture

**Theological anchor:** The Hebraic concept of *Binah* (understanding) -- the ability to distinguish one thing from another. You cannot understand what you haven't first categorized.

**Output:** A structured outline or map with categories and governing questions, details intentionally left blank.

---

## Day 3 -- Dry Land and Vegetation: Establish Foundations That Bear Fruit

**Genesis principle:** Land appears and immediately produces vegetation -- living, generative material. Foundations aren't inert; they produce.

**Learning operation: First-principles study**

- For each category from Day 2, identify the **1-2 foundational concepts** that everything else grows from
- Study these foundations deeply -- textbook, lecture, primary sources
- For each foundation, articulate it in your own words (the "Feynman test": if you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it)
- Write your own definitions, not the textbook's

**Theological anchor:** Matthew 7:24-25 -- building on rock vs. sand. First principles are the bedrock. If your foundations are wrong, everything built on them collapses.

**Output:** Your own written explanations of the core concepts in each category. Simple language. No jargon you can't define.

---

## Day 4 -- Sun, Moon, and Stars: Install Governing Rules

**Genesis principle:** The light-bearers *govern* day and night. They don't just exist -- they rule, regulate, and provide orientation. This is Day 1's domain (light/dark) now receiving its **rulers**.

**Learning operation: Map the rules, laws, and frameworks**

- For each category, identify the **governing principles** -- the rules that regulate how things work within that domain
- These are formulas, heuristics, cause-effect relationships, decision rules, taxonomies
- Create **reference cards**: one card per governing principle, stated as a rule ("When X, then Y" / "A is to B as C is to D")
- Connect each rule back to the foundation it grows from (Day 3)

**Theological anchor:** Psalm 119:105 -- "Your word is a lamp to my feet." Governing principles illuminate the path through complex material. Without them, you're navigating in the dark.

**Output:** A set of rule cards or a reference sheet linking principles to their foundations.

---

## Day 5 -- Birds and Fish: Let Details Multiply

**Genesis principle:** God fills the sky and sea with creatures and commands them to "be fruitful and multiply." The domains are now **populated** abundantly.

**Learning operation: Active detail acquisition**

- Now fill your scaffold (Day 2) with specifics: examples, cases, data, evidence, applications
- Use **active recall**: close the book, write what you remember, check, repeat
- Practice problems, case studies, past exam questions -- anything that forces *retrieval* not just re-reading
- Let knowledge "multiply" by generating your own examples and analogies

**Theological anchor:** The *Shema* learning model (Deuteronomy 6:6-9) -- internalize, then repeat in every context: "when you sit, when you walk, when you lie down, when you rise." Repetition across varied contexts is how knowledge multiplies.

**Output:** Filled-in scaffold. Practice problems attempted. Your own examples written.

---

## Day 6 -- Dominion: Apply, Name, and Teach

**Genesis principle:** Humans are created to exercise *dominion* -- to name the animals, to work the garden, to actively steward creation. Knowledge isn't knowledge until you can **use** it.

**Learning operation: Application and teaching**

- **Apply:** Work through complex problems, real-world scenarios, or creative applications that require combining multiple concepts
- **Name:** Create your own terminology, mnemonics, or mental models that make the material *yours*
- **Teach:** Explain the material to someone else (or write it as if teaching). The act of teaching is the highest form of learning -- it exposes every gap

**Theological anchor:** Genesis 2:19 -- Adam naming the animals was an act of *authority through understanding*. You have dominion over what you can name. If you can't articulate it, you don't own it.

**Output:** Completed application problems. A short "teach-back" -- written or spoken explanation as if to a peer who knows nothing.

---

## Day 7 -- Sabbath Rest: Consolidate and Release

**Genesis principle:** God rested -- not from exhaustion, but because the work was *complete*. Rest is the final act that seals creation.

**Learning operation: Consolidation**

- **No new input.** Do not study new material.
- Review your outputs from Days 1-6 briefly (15-20 min max)
- Identify any remaining gaps and note them for the next cycle
- Sleep is when memory consolidation physically happens -- protect it
- Engage in unrelated activity: exercise, nature, worship, conversation. The subconscious processes what the conscious mind has loaded.

**Theological anchor:** Hebrews 4:9-11 -- rest is an act of trust and completion. Overloading without consolidation is like planting without letting the rain fall. Ecclesiastes 3:1 -- there is a time for every purpose. The time for rest is not wasted time.

**Output:** A brief reflection noting what stuck, what didn't, and what the next cycle should target.

---

## Cycle Summary

| Day | Genesis Act | Learning Operation | Key Output |
|-----|-----------|-------------------|------------|
| 1 | Light from darkness | Epistemic triage | Known/unknown map |
| 2 | Firmament (structure) | Build scaffold | Category outline with governing questions |
| 3 | Land and vegetation | First-principles study | Own-words explanations of foundations |
| 4 | Sun, moon, stars | Governing rules | Rule cards linked to foundations |
| 5 | Birds and fish | Active detail acquisition | Filled scaffold, practice, own examples |
| 6 | Humans (dominion) | Apply, name, teach | Application work + teach-back |
| 7 | Sabbath rest | Consolidation | Reflection + next-cycle targets |

---

## Compounding Cycles

Each new cycle begins where the last one ended. Day 7's reflection becomes the next Day 1's triage. Over multiple cycles:

- **Cycle 1:** Survey-level understanding. You can explain the big picture.
- **Cycle 2:** Working knowledge. You can solve problems and answer questions.
- **Cycle 3:** Mastery. You can teach, apply creatively, and handle edge cases.

The creation pattern is not linear -- it is **spiraling**. Each pass deepens what came before, just as the biblical narrative itself spirals (Genesis 1 gives the overview, Genesis 2 deepens Day 6).
