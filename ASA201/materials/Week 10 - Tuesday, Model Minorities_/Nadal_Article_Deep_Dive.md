# Deep Dive: "How Neoliberalism Remade the Model Minority Myth" by Paul Nadal

## Basic Info
**Published**: 2023 (very recent!)
**Author**: Paul Nadal
**Journal**: *Representations* (UC Press)

## The Key Players (Economists)

### Theodore W. Schultz
- Nobel Prize-winning economist (1979)
- Chicago School of Economics
- Wrote about Japan in 1959 essay "Human Wealth and Economic Growth"
- Used Japan's post-WWII economic miracle as proof that education = economic investment
- Argued public education created "human capital" that drove growth

### Gary Becker
- Nobel Prize-winning economist (1992)
- Chicago School of Economics
- Wrote *Human Capital* (1964) and *A Treatise on the Family* (1981)
- Went to China in 1981 and used Chinese families to develop theory of "endowments"
- Turned Asian families into economic models

## What's Nadal's Overall Stance?

**NADAL IS VERY CRITICAL - HE COMPLETELY OPPOSES HUMAN CAPITAL THEORY**

The author is NOT supporting these economists. He's exposing how they:
1. Used Asian examples to make racist ideas seem "scientific"
2. Turned human beings into economic investments
3. Used Asian success to argue against welfare programs and social spending
4. Built neoliberal economics on racializing Asian families

## The Core Argument

**Here's what Nadal shows these economists did:**

### Schultz's Move (1950s-60s):
- Looked at Japan's rapid economic growth after WWII
- Said: "See? They invested in education and got rich!"
- Used this to argue education should be viewed as economic investment, not a right
- Turned Japanese students into proof that "human capital" drives growth
- **BUT** - he ignored US aid to Japan, Cold War geopolitics, Korean War boom, etc.
- He cherry-picked Japan to make his theory look correct

### Becker's Move (1970s-80s):
- Went to China and studied family structures
- Developed theory of "endowments" - parents pass biological, health, and cultural capital to children
- Used Asian families (tiger parents, educational pressure) as proof that families are "firms" making rational investments
- Argued: If Asian families can invest in kids without government help, we don't need welfare
- **Key quote from your pages (p. 90)**: Asian success shows "a rate of return on educational investments that, in principle, could only be posited retroactively" - meaning it only works backwards, it's not actually predictive, but they used it anyway

### The Racist Logic:
- **Page 91**: Becker used Asian success as proof of "a new form of capitalism but on a tried-and-true recipe" while also calling China a "negative" counterexample
- They positioned Asians as BOTH "ideal" (model) AND "threat" (yellow peril)
- **Page 92**: "Asian family forms mediated the analogy between the microeconomics of the home and the macroeconomics of education"
- They turned Asian families into economic models to justify cutting social programs

## Does Human Capital Theory Help or Hurt?

**NADAL ARGUES IT DEEPLY HARMS:**

1. **It erases structural racism**: By saying "education = investment = success", it ignores discrimination, exclusion, redlining, etc.

2. **It turns people into capital**: Literally - you're not a person, you're an investment. Your value is your economic productivity.

3. **It justifies inequality**: If Asians can succeed through "family investment," then Black/Latino communities just need to "invest more" - ignoring completely different histories and barriers.

4. **It's used against social programs**: "Why do we need welfare/affirmative action if Asians made it without help?"

5. **It racializes Asians as economic agents**: Not humans with full lives, but walking economic models.

## The Cold War Context (CRUCIAL)

From page 84-85 and the Schultz archival document:
- Schultz was obsessed with beating the Soviet Union economically
- He used Asian examples to argue against socialism
- Human capital theory emerged as anti-communist propaganda
- **The archive image (p. 84)**: Schultz literally crossing out comparisons to the USSR but suggesting it might be doing better than the US - this terrified him

**Page 93**: "Human capital theory placed the modern university at the center of these debates, arguing that its expansion in the US provided 'a necessary weapon' against China and the Soviet Union."

## What's Really Going On? (The Genius of This Article)

Nadal is showing that **human capital theory didn't just describe Asian success - it CREATED the modern version of the model minority myth.**

Before Chicago economists:
- Model minority = cultural stereotype

After Chicago economists:
- Model minority = economic "proof" embedded in Nobel Prize-winning theories
- Now it looks scientific, not racist
- It became mainstream economic common sense

**The kicker (page 92-93)**: Nadal shows this was "theoretical utopianism" - it wasn't based on real data, it was ideological. Schultz admitted "it takes years as a rule to develop these capabilities **and then it requires more years** for these capabilities to prove their worth in production."

It's circular reasoning dressed up as economics.

## Why This Matters Today

This is happening RIGHT NOW:
- Debates about Asian Americans and affirmative action use this logic
- "Tiger parenting" discourse treats families as investment firms
- Arguments against welfare cite Asian success
- College admissions controversies frame Asians as hyper-successful investors in education

**Nadal's point**: All of this traces back to neoliberal economists in the 1950s-80s who used Asian examples to build theories that justify inequality and cut social programs.

## Author's Bottom Line

Human capital theory is:
1. Racist (uses Asian racialization as foundation)
2. Dehumanizing (turns people into investments)
3. Politically motivated (Cold War anti-communism)
4. Not actually scientific (cherry-picked examples, circular logic)
5. Actively harmful (used to justify cutting programs that help marginalized communities)

**The model minority myth isn't just a cultural stereotype - it's embedded in the foundational theories of neoliberal economics.**

That's why this article is so important and so radical.
