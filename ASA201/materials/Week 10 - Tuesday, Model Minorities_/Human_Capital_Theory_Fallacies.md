# The Mathematical Fallacies of Human Capital Theory

## The Base Equation Again:

**HC = Investment × Rate of Return**

Or more formally:

**PV(Earnings) = Σ [Investment_t × R_t / (1+r)^t]**

Where:
- PV = Present Value of lifetime earnings
- Investment_t = Education/training investment at time t
- R_t = Rate of return at time t
- r = discount rate

---

## The 7 Fatal Flaws (Technical/Empirical)

### 1. **THE ENDOGENEITY PROBLEM** (Most Critical)

**The issue:**
- Does Investment → Earnings? (causal)
- Or do Earnings → Investment? (reverse causality)
- Or does some third factor cause BOTH?

**Example with Asian families:**
- Becker says: Asian families invest in education → high earnings
- But maybe: Wealthy Asian families can afford more education AND their wealth/networks drive earnings
- **Correlation ≠ Causation**

**Why this is fatal:**
- Can't establish causal direction
- Makes the entire theory untestable
- Like saying "successful people make good decisions" - how do you know? They succeeded!

**Parallel to EMH:**
- EMH says: Price reflects all information
- But does information → price? Or does price movement → information interpretation?
- 2008 showed housing prices went up, people THEN justified it with "information"

### 2. **THE MEASUREMENT PROBLEM** (Circular Definition)

**The issue:**
You can ONLY measure "Human Capital" by looking at earnings outcomes.

**The equation claims:**
- HC causes Earnings
- But you measure HC by... Earnings

**This is circular:**
1. Why does Person A earn more than Person B?
2. "Because Person A has more Human Capital"
3. How do you know Person A has more HC?
4. "Because they earn more"
5. **You've explained nothing**

**Like saying:**
- "This medicine makes people healthy"
- "How do you measure if someone has taken the medicine?"
- "They're healthy"
- **Circular**

**Technical term:** This is an **identification problem** - you can't separately identify the variable you claim is causal

### 3. **OMITTED VARIABLE BIAS** (The Structural Factors)

**What the equation IGNORES:**

**Earnings = f(HC Investment) + ERROR**

**But ERROR contains:**
- Discrimination (race, gender, age)
- Social networks and connections
- Family wealth (beyond educational investment)
- Geographic location and local labor markets
- Historical advantages/disadvantages
- Luck and timing
- Employer bias and hiring practices
- **Immigration selection effects** (crucial for Asians!)

**The Asian immigration example:**
- Post-1965 Immigration Act selected for highly educated Asian immigrants
- These weren't random Asians - they were already elite in their home countries
- Their children's success reflects initial selection, not just "investment"
- **Selection bias makes the whole model invalid**

**Technical problem:**
If omitted variables are correlated with your independent variable (Investment), your coefficient estimates are BIASED.

**In plain English:**
You think you're measuring the effect of investment, but you're actually measuring investment + networks + discrimination + selection + everything else.

### 4. **THE RATE OF RETURN IS NOT CONSTANT** (Heterogeneous Treatment Effects)

**The equation assumes:**
$1 of investment → Same return for everyone

**But empirically:**
- Asian with BA: Return = X
- Black with BA: Return = X - discrimination penalty
- Woman with BA: Return = X - gender wage gap
- **Same investment, different returns**

**This breaks the model:**
If R varies systematically by race/gender, then success ISN'T just about investment.

**Graph this:**
```
Same Education Investment →
Asian: High earnings
Black: Lower earnings (for SAME degree)
→ This ISN'T human capital difference
→ This IS structural racism
```

**The equation can't handle this** because it assumes R is constant or varies randomly, not systematically by race.

### 5. **NO EQUILIBRIUM CONDITION** (Unlike EMH)

**EMH at least has a theoretical equilibrium:**
- If price is too high, people sell → price drops
- If price is too low, people buy → price rises
- Market moves toward equilibrium (in theory)

**Human Capital Theory has NO such mechanism:**
- If everyone invests in education, what happens?
- Do returns stay constant? (No - credential inflation)
- Does the "return" adjust? (Yes, but the theory doesn't model this)
- **No equilibrium, no self-correcting mechanism**

**Real-world example:**
- 1970s: College degree = high returns
- 2020s: Everyone has college degree = credential inflation = lower returns
- **But HCT doesn't predict this or explain it**

**Technical issue:**
Without equilibrium conditions, you can't make predictions. The model has no dynamic mechanism.

### 6. **THE LUCAS CRITIQUE / POLICY INVARIANCE**

**Named after economist Robert Lucas (Chicago School ironically!):**

**The problem:**
When you change policy based on a model, people's behavior changes, which breaks the model.

**Applied to Human Capital:**
- Theory says: Invest in education → get returns
- Policy: Subsidize education massively
- Result: Everyone gets educated → credential inflation → returns drop
- **The relationship you modeled no longer holds**

**This makes HCT USELESS for policy:**
- You can't use it to predict what happens if you change education policy
- Because the returns themselves will change
- The model is not "policy invariant"

### 7. **THE SURVIVORSHIP BIAS**

**The issue:**
Theory built on studying successful groups (Japan, Asian Americans).

**But:**
- How many countries invested in education and DIDN'T grow? (Not studied)
- How many Asian families invested heavily and DIDN'T succeed? (Not studied)
- **You only see the winners, not the losers**

**Like studying:**
- Successful entrepreneurs to learn business
- Ignoring all the failures who did the same things
- Concluding "just do what successful people did"
- **Ignoring that many who did the same things failed**

**Technical term:** **Sample selection bias** - your sample (Japan, Asian Americans) isn't representative

## How These Errors Lead to "Crashes" (Policy Failures)

### Like EMH → 2008 Crisis, HCT → Policy Disasters

**1. College Debt Crisis**
- HCT says: Education = investment with positive ROI
- Policy: Make college loans easy (rational investment!)
- Result: $1.7 trillion in student debt, many degrees don't pay off
- **Model failed because returns aren't constant and vary by field/person**

**2. "Just Learn to Code" Policies**
- HCT logic: Invest in tech skills → high returns
- Policy: Push everyone into coding bootcamps
- Result: Credential inflation, tech layoffs, doesn't solve structural unemployment
- **Model failed because supply/demand dynamics not modeled**

**3. Cutting Social Programs**
- HCT logic: Success = investment, poverty = lack of investment
- Policy: Cut welfare, assume people will "invest" instead
- Result: Deepening poverty, because structural barriers still exist
- **Model failed because omitted variable bias (racism, discrimination)**

**4. "Model Minority" Policies**
- HCT logic: Asians succeeded through family investment, others can too
- Policy: Reduce support for other minorities
- Result: Ignores completely different histories, immigration selection
- **Model failed because selection bias and different structural contexts**

## Summary: The Core Fallacies

**The equation HC = Investment × Return ASSUMES:**
- Investment causes returns (endogeneity)
- You can measure HC independently (you can't - circular)
- Returns are constant across groups (they're not - discrimination)
- No other factors matter (omitted variable bias)
- Selected winners are representative (survivorship bias)

**These aren't minor technical issues - they're FATAL FLAWS that make the theory:**
- Untestable (can't falsify)
- Unpredictive (can't forecast)
- Circular (defines success by success)
- Biased (ignores structural factors)

**Yet it won Nobel Prizes and drives policy because:**
- The math looks rigorous
- It justifies cutting social programs
- It makes inequality look natural
- **Rigor provides cover for ideology**

**Just like EMH.**
