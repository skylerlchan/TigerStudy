One major ramification of self-orientalizing for Asian American identity is that it makes the stereotype harder to resist. Asian Americans lose their ability to simply reject this narrative, as it is "constructed by the East and West alike." Another aspect of American imperialism is fixing backwardness, and Techno-Orientalism effectively does this because it has a "great emphasis on the project of modernity — cultures privilege modernity and fear losing their perceived “edge” over others." Asian Americans may feel that this narrative binds them into a box they can't escape. As Japan and China market themselves as tech powerhouses, this places an image on Asian Americans as being good at STEM or working in tech.

Perhaps the most serious ramification is historical, however, and stems from the same racist undertones of Orientalism. Just as coolies were described as expendable workers, the "U.S. techno-Orientalist imagination has its roots in the view of the Asian body — the Chinese body, most specifically — as a form of expendable technology." This unknowingly reinforces century-old dehumanizing views on Asian Americans.

Even among Asian countries, there is a level of Western order that subjugates one country to another. While both are economic powerhouses, "to put it in starker terms, Japan creates technology, but China is the technology." In this sense, Asian Americans are being sorted into their own stereotypes. For instance, some are seen as innovators, while others are categorized as manufacturers. Nevertheless, both are reduced to their relationship with technology rather than being seen as humans with diverse interests and identities. Thus, when Asian Americans self-orientalize for economic gains, they lose their ability to control their own narrative and hence inadvertently validate their own limitations.
