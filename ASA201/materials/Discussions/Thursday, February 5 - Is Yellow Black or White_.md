**Ethan Do** (2026-02-05):

Okihiro’s answer to the question is that “yellow” is neither black nor white. Historically, Asians have been placed in an in between space reinforced by white supremacy. He also suggests that Asians and Black Americans share similar experiences in subjugation, but differ in reasons why. “Yellow is emphatically neither white nor black, but insofar as Asians and Africans share a subordinate position to the master class, yellow is a shade of black, and black, a shade of yellow” (34).  

Okihiro proves that the racial identity of Asian Americans was not fixed or natural but was legally altered over time to sustain white supremacy. For example, prior to 1870, Chinese Americans were considered white. However, this was not always the case. By 1900, if they had two Chinese parents, they were considered Chinese. But if they were of mixed race, they were considered either black or white. Therefore, despite being legally categorized with whites, they were denied all the rights and privileges of being white. In the case of Gong Lum v. Rice (1927), the Supreme Court of the United States ruled that a Chinese girl was not allowed into a white school. She was deemed “colored.” According to Okihiro, “Chinese were nonwhite and hence ‘colored’ and thus could be barred from schools reserved for whites” (p. 53). Thus, it is clear that racial identity was historically dependent on white supremacy and was not based on biological or historical facts. 

Okihiro points to an important moment of Afro-Asian solidarity in the face of racism, which came when the Brotherhood of Sleeping Car Porters, under the leadership of A. Philip Randolph, accepted the Filipino workers into their union during the 1930s. Rather than seeing the Filipino workers as competition, the union saw the common cause and declared “We want our Filipino brothers to understand that it is necessary… to help secure conditions and wages which they too will benefit from” (p. 54). A similar situation arose during the 2020 George Floyd protests and the StopAsianHate movement, where Black and Asian groups came together in solidarity
