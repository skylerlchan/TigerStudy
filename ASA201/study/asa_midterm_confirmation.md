## Analysis Approach

### Primary Theoretical Framework: Differential Inclusion

**Definition:**
Differential inclusion describes how marginalized groups gain access to dominant institutions and mainstream visibility—but only on limited terms that don't threaten existing power structures. You're included, BUT with conditions attached.

For Asian Americans in media, differential inclusion means:
- Representation is granted IF anger is removed
- Stories are told IF they confirm the American dream
- Cultural difference is shown IF it's "cute" not threatening
- Racism can be depicted IF it's individual and solvable, not structural and ongoing

Unlike outright exclusion (no Asian Americans on TV at all), differential inclusion offers limited access while setting strict boundaries on what can be said, shown, or critiqued.

**How This Operates:**
Differential inclusion works through what appears to be progress. Networks can say "we're diverse!" while actually maintaining control over:
- What narratives are "televisable"
- Which versions of Asian American life are acceptable
- How racism can (and cannot) be represented
- What gets sanitized, softened, or removed entirely

The mechanism is subtle: inclusion itself becomes the tool for managing and containing difference, rather than transforming power relations.

**Application to Fresh Off the Boat:**
The show is a textbook case of differential inclusion in action.

**What was INCLUDED (the terms of access):**
- Asian American family on prime-time network TV
- Chinese cultural markers (food, language, references)
- Immigration narrative
- Economic struggle (restaurant ownership)

**What was REQUIRED for inclusion (the conditions):**
- Remove Eddie Huang's anger and profanity from the memoir
- Transform violent racism into "quirky misunderstandings"
- Make conflicts resolvable in 22 minutes
- Add laugh track to make racism "funny" not threatening
- Confirm that hard work leads to success (American dream ideology)
- Focus on family harmony over structural critique

**What this reveals:**
The gap between memoir and show exposes the terms of differential inclusion. ABC didn't exclude Asian Americans—they included them conditionally. The adaptation process required stripping away everything that would challenge white viewers or critique American power structures.

The studio gets progressive credit for representation while actually maintaining control over how Asian Americans can be seen.
