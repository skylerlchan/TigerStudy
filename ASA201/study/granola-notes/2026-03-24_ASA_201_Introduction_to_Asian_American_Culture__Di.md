<h3>Techno-Orientalism: Core Concepts &amp; Analysis</h3>
<ul>
<li>Definition and framework
<ul>
<li>Imagining Asia/Asians in hypo/hypertechnological terms in cultural productions and political discourse</li>
<li>Differs from traditional orientalism by positioning Asia as technologically advanced rather than backward</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Key manifestations
<ul>
<li>Japan portrayed as innovator vs China as manufacturer</li>
<li>Innovation less feared than manufacturing capacity</li>
<li>Dehumanizes Asian populations as manufacturing potential rather than human beings</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Bidirectional nature
<ul>
<li>Not just Western tool against Asia</li>
<li>Asian nations self-orientalize for strategic advantage</li>
<li>Japan embraces “cyberpunk” identity to justify superiority (nihon jinron concept)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Counter-dialogue potential
<ul>
<li>Asian creators can reclaim tropes to fight dehumanization</li>
<li>Debate over effectiveness: can “master’s tools” dismantle the system?</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
