### Key Theoretical Concepts

- Disidentification (José Muñoz theory)
- Taking cultural objects not meant for you and transforming them- Marginalized people reappropriating hostile culture for survival/pleasure- Examples: drag performances, camp readings of mainstream media
- Landscape as cultural practice (W.J.T. Mitchell)
- Not just what landscape means, but what it does- Functions to establish belonging, justify settler colonialism- Denaturalizing “natural” scenes as artificial constructions
- Abstract labor under capitalism
- All individual skills reduced to generic “Labor”- Race reintroduced to divide and exploit workers- Chinese workers’ different “socially necessary labor time”

### Historical Context: Railroad Colonialism

- Chinese laborers built westward railroad expansion
- Characterized as requiring less sustenance, no families
- Seen as robotic, non-human “alien class”
- White settlers used landscape imagery to move closer to indigenous position
- Opposed to “abstract forces of capital” represented by Asian workers
- Triangulation: white settler, Asian worker, indigenous peoples
