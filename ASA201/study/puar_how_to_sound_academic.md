# How to Talk About Puar Like You Study This Stuff

## Quick Translation Guide: What They Mean vs. What You Say

### Core Concepts - ELI5 to Academic

| What It Actually Means | How to Say It Academically |
|------------------------|----------------------------|
| Some gay people get included in nationalism now | "We're seeing the emergence of **homonationalism**, where certain homosexual subjects are folded into nationalist formations" |
| Being "transgressive" has become its own rule | "Puar interrogates how queerness operates as a **regulatory script** even as it claims transgression" |
| White gay people get privileges by distancing from Muslims | "Queer liberal subjects accrue legitimacy through **Orientalist constructions** of Muslim sexuality" |
| You can't be both properly queer and religious (according to mainstream queerness) | "**Queer secularity** presumes an incompatibility between religious devotion and queer agency" |
| The US uses gay rights to justify imperialism | "U.S. **sexual exceptionalism** functions as a biopolitical project that legitimates imperial expansion" |

## Essential Vocabulary (Use These Constantly)

### Verbs That Make You Sound Smart
- **Interrogate** (not "question") - "Puar interrogates the binary between assimilation and transgression"
- **Problematize** (not "show problems with") - "We need to problematize the secular assumptions in queer theory"
- **Foreground** (not "emphasize") - "She foregrounds the regulatory dimensions of queerness"
- **Gesture toward** (not "suggest") - "This gestures toward a more complex understanding"
- **Trouble** (not "complicate") - "How does this trouble our assumptions about agency?"
- **Implicate** (not "involve") - "Queerness is implicated in projects of whiteness"
- **Articulate** (not "express") - "Puar articulates a critique of homonormative formations"
- **Animate** (not "drive") - "What desires animate queer liberal politics?"
- **Sutured to** (not "connected to") - "Queer exceptionalism is sutured to American empire"
- **Enact** (not "do") - "This discourse enacts violence through claims to liberation"

### Key Phrases to Drop
- "**Reading against the grain**" - finding alternative interpretations
- "**Productive tensions**" - when contradictions are actually useful
- "**Normativizing forces**" - things that create norms
- "**The biopolitical stakes**" - life/death consequences
- "**Regimes of truth**" (Foucault) - what counts as "true" in power structures
- "**Epistemic violence**" - when knowledge systems harm people
- "**Affective economies**" - how emotions circulate and create value
- "**Assemblages**" (Puar's thing) - complex arrangements where identity isn't fixed

## Sentence Starters for Class Discussion

### When You Agree
- "Building on that point, I'm thinking about how..."
- "That resonates with Puar's argument about..."
- "Right, and what's particularly generative about that is..."
- "I want to stay with that idea for a moment because..."

### When You Want to Complicate Something
- "I wonder if we might also consider..."
- "That's compelling, though I'm troubled by..."
- "What remains unresolved for me is..."
- "Can we sit with the tension between..."
- "I'm curious about what gets elided when we frame it that way..."

### When You're Not Sure But Want to Sound Thoughtful
- "I'm still working through this, but it seems like..."
- "There's something productive in the friction between..."
- "I want to hold space for both readings here..."
- "The text seems to gesture toward... but doesn't fully articulate..."

### When Someone Says Something Kinda Problematic
- "I appreciate that reading, but I wonder if it risks recentering..."
- "That's an important intervention, though we might attend to how..."
- "Can we pause on the assumptions embedded in that claim?"

## How to Discuss Queer Secularity in Class

### Basic Points to Make

**Point 1: The Secular Assumption**
"What's striking about queer secularity is how it operates as an unmarked norm. The presumption is that the queer agential subject can only be fathomed *outside* religious frames—as if religiosity necessarily forecloses sexual agency. This is particularly violent when mapped onto Muslim subjects."

**Point 2: The Either/Or Trap**
"Puar's discussion of queer Muslims reveals an impossible double bind. They're positioned within an either/or framework: you're either queer (secular, modern, liberated) or Muslim (traditional, repressed, unfree). The possibility of queer religiosity is rendered unintelligible within this logic."

**Point 3: Secularism as Western Imperialism**
"Queer secularity functions as a form of imperial exportation—it positions western secular queerness as the universal model and then reads non-western religious contexts as necessarily homophobic. This actually reproduces colonial logics under the guise of LGBTQ liberation."

## How to Discuss Regulatory Queerness in Class

### The Core Argument (In Academic Speak)

"Puar's most provocative intervention is her interrogation of queerness itself as a regulatory formation. She argues that queerness, precisely through its claims to be anti-normative and transgressive, becomes a biopolitical regulatory apparatus. The demand for 'freedom from norms' operates as its own norm that disciplines queer subjects."

### Three Angles to Discuss

**Angle 1: Transgression as Discipline**
"The idealization of transgression actually enacts its own forms of regulation. If transgression becomes the measure of 'proper' queerness, we've simply substituted one normativity for another. Moreover, this privileges certain subjects—typically white, secular, cosmopolitan—who have access to transgression as lifestyle."

**Angle 2: Queerness as Alibi**
"When queerness is positioned as inherently resistant, it can function as an alibi for complicity with other power structures. Being queer becomes a kind of get-out-of-jail-free card—like, 'I'm already marginalized, so I can't be implicated in racism or nationalism.' But Puar shows how queerness can be sutured to projects of whiteness and empire."

**Angle 3: Freedom's Exclusions**
"The rhetoric of individual freedom that animates queer liberal politics actually depends on the production of unfree others. To cite Sara Ahmed, whom Puar engages, 'The idealization of movement depends on the exclusion of others who are already positioned as not free in the same way.' Queer cosmopolitanism requires immobile, unfree subjects to define itself against."

## Discussion Questions You Can Ask (Sound Smart AF)

1. "How might we think about queer agency outside the secular/religious binary that structures so much queer theory?"

2. "What are the biopolitical stakes of defining queerness through transgression? Who lives, who dies in that formulation?"

3. "Can we identify moments where queer politics become complicit with nationalist or imperial projects—and what would it mean to remain attentive to those complicities without falling into paralysis?"

4. "How does the ascendancy of whiteness operate through, not despite, the incorporation of some queer subjects into the body politic?"

5. "If we take seriously Puar's critique of regulatory queerness, what would a non-regulatory queer politics look like? Is that even possible?"

6. "Where do we see homonationalism operating in contemporary LGBTQ movements—Pride, marriage equality discourse, representations of 'gay-friendly' nations vs. 'homophobic' ones?"

## Sample Responses for Common Discussion Topics

### If Someone Talks About Gay Rights as Universal Progress

"That's an important point, but I'm thinking about how that narrative of universal progress might be doing ideological work. Puar would argue that positioning the West as the site of sexual modernity actually reproduces Orientalist logics and can justify imperial interventions. Like, when we say 'gay rights are human rights,' we need to interrogate *whose* version of gay rights, and how that formulation might erase non-western queer formations that don't map onto liberal rights frameworks."

### If Someone Talks About Coming Out Narratives

"Yeah, and Puar troubles the teleology embedded in coming-out narratives. The assumption is that the closet is pure repression and coming out is liberation, but that privileges a particular kind of individualistic, secular subject. For queer folks embedded in religious communities, or immigrant communities, or communities of color, that binary doesn't hold. Sometimes staying closeted *is* an agentic choice, not a failure of modernity."

### If Someone Talks About Religion as Homophobic

"I want to push back on that framing a bit. When we position religion—especially Islam—as inherently or uniquely homophobic, we're enacting the queer secularity that Puar critiques. It mistakes state repression for sexual repression and denies any agency to queer Muslims. Like, queer religiosity becomes unthinkable within this logic, which actually forecloses possibilities for queer life that don't conform to western secular models."

### If Someone Talks About Intersectionality

"Absolutely, and I think Puar both builds on and complicates intersectional frameworks. She's attentive to how even queer of color critique can become regulatory—like, holding queer of color subjects to impossible standards of perfect resistance, or positioning them as always-already beyond reproach. Intersectionality is crucial, but we also need to remain vigilant about how any framework can become normativizing."

## Red Flags to Avoid (Don't Say These)

❌ "I think..." → ✓ "I'm thinking about how..."
❌ "This is wrong" → ✓ "I want to trouble that claim"
❌ "Obviously..." → ✓ "What becomes visible when we..."
❌ "Everyone knows" → ✓ "The dominant discourse suggests"
❌ "This proves" → ✓ "This might gesture toward"
❌ "The author says" → ✓ "Puar articulates/argues/theorizes"

## Power Moves for Seminar

### The Close Reading
Pick a single sentence from Puar and unpack it for 2 minutes.
"Can we sit with this sentence for a moment? When Puar writes [quote], there's a lot of density here. The term 'regulatory' signals Foucauldian biopower, but she's applying it to queerness itself, which is the radical move..."

### The "What Gets Elided" Move
"That's a compelling reading, but I'm curious about what gets elided when we frame it that way. What subjects or possibilities become unintelligible?"

### The Tension Hold
"I want to hold the tension between these two claims rather than resolving it. There's something productive in sitting with contradiction here."

### The Biopolitics Escalation
Whenever possible, raise the stakes to life/death.
"What are the biopolitical stakes of this? Who is marked for life versus death in this formulation?"

## Final Tips

1. **Always historicize** - don't treat things as natural, show they have histories
2. **Stay with tension** - don't resolve contradictions too quickly
3. **Name your stakes** - why does this matter? Who lives, who dies?
4. **Be generous, then critical** - "That's generative, AND I want to push on..."
5. **Use the passive voice strategically** - "What gets obscured..." "How is X produced..."
6. **Question your own position** - show reflexivity about your complicity

Remember: In gender/sexuality studies, it's good to be uncertain, to wrestle with ideas, to acknowledge complexity. You're not trying to have The Answer—you're trying to think critically about power, norms, and violence.
