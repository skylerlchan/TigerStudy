# Thelonious Monk: Annotated Scholarly Bibliography

*Compiled February 27, 2026 -- Sources for an MLA-format college jazz history paper*

---

## 1. Major Biographies

### Robin D.G. Kelley -- *Thelonious Monk: The Life and Times of an American Original*

- **Author:** Robin D.G. Kelley
- **Title:** *Thelonious Monk: The Life and Times of an American Original*
- **Publisher:** Free Press (Simon & Schuster)
- **Year:** 2009
- **Pages:** 588
- **ISBN:** 978-1439190463
- **Description:** The definitive scholarly biography, based on over fourteen years of research including access to the Monk family's private archives, previously unseen documents, and extensive interviews. Kelley, a distinguished historian at UCLA, covers Monk's entire life from his North Carolina birth through his Minton's Playhouse years, his Blue Note and Riverside recordings, his 1964 *Time* magazine cover, and his final reclusive years. Widely considered the single most important book on Monk.
- **URL:** [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Thelonious-Monk-Times-American-Original/dp/1439190461)

### Leslie Gourse -- *Straight, No Chaser: The Life and Genius of Thelonious Monk*

- **Author:** Leslie Gourse
- **Title:** *Straight, No Chaser: The Life and Genius of Thelonious Monk*
- **Publisher:** Schirmer Books (Macmillan)
- **Year:** 1997
- **Pages:** 384
- **ISBN:** 978-0028646015
- **Description:** A narrative biography drawing on extensive interviews with musicians, family, and associates. Gourse was a prolific jazz biographer (she also wrote books on Ella Fitzgerald, Billie Holiday, and others), and this book provides a readable, well-researched portrait of Monk's career, personality, and mental health struggles. It was the most comprehensive Monk biography available before Kelley's 2009 work.

### Thomas Fitterling -- *Thelonious Monk: His Life and Music*

- **Author:** Thomas Fitterling; translated from the German by Robert Dobbin
- **Title:** *Thelonious Monk: His Life and Music*
- **Publisher:** Berkeley Hills Books
- **Year:** 1997 (English translation; German original published 1987)
- **Pages:** 240
- **ISBN:** 978-0876390481
- **Description:** Originally published in Germany as *Thelonious Monk: Sein Leben, seine Musik, seine Schallplatten* (1987), this was the first full-length Monk biography. Fitterling combines a biographical narrative with musicological analysis and a detailed discography. Particularly useful for its systematic treatment of Monk's recordings and musical style.

### Laurent de Wilde -- *Monk*

- **Author:** Laurent de Wilde; translated from the French by Jonathan Dickinson
- **Title:** *Monk*
- **Publisher:** Marlowe & Company
- **Year:** 1997 (English translation; French original published 1996 by Gallimard)
- **Pages:** 256
- **ISBN:** 978-1569247532
- **Description:** Written by a French jazz pianist, this biography is distinctive for its perspective as a fellow musician. De Wilde brings a performer's insight into Monk's harmonic and rhythmic language, offering analysis grounded in the experience of actually playing Monk's compositions. Winner of the Prix Litteraire de la Fondation SJA in France.

## 2. Other Scholarly Books on Monk

### Gabriel Solis -- *Monk's Music: Thelonious Monk and Jazz History in the Making*

- **Author:** Gabriel Solis
- **Title:** *Monk's Music: Thelonious Monk and Jazz History in the Making*
- **Publisher:** University of Minnesota Press
- **Year:** 2007
- **Pages:** 248
- **ISBN:** 978-0816649723
- **Description:** An academic study examining how Monk's music has been interpreted, reinterpreted, and canonized across generations of jazz musicians. Solis, a professor of musicology and ethnomusicology at the University of Illinois, focuses on how younger musicians have engaged with Monk's repertoire and what this reveals about jazz tradition-making. A key scholarly text for understanding Monk's cultural legacy and reception history.
- **URL:** [Goodreads listing](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/2101289-monk-s-music)

### Rob van der Bliek (editor) -- *The Thelonious Monk Reader*

- **Author/Editor:** Rob van der Bliek, editor
- **Title:** *The Thelonious Monk Reader*
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press
- **Year:** 2001
- **Pages:** 288
- **ISBN:** 978-0195304831
- **Description:** An anthology of the most important criticism, profiles, interviews, and essays written about Monk from the 1940s through the 1990s. Includes pieces by Nat Hentoff, Ira Gitler, Martin Williams, Whitney Balliett, and others. Essential for understanding how critical perception of Monk evolved over decades. Part of Oxford's "Readers on American Musicians" series.

### Charley Gerard -- *Thelonious Monk: Originals and Standards*

- **Author:** Charley Gerard
- **Title:** *Thelonious Monk: Originals and Standards*
- **Publisher:** Hal Leonard (originally self-published, 1997)
- **Year:** 1997 (revised edition)
- **Description:** A music-theory-oriented study that analyzes Monk's compositions and his interpretations of jazz standards. Gerard provides lead sheets, harmonic analysis, and discussion of Monk's idiosyncratic approach to melody, rhythm, and dissonance. Useful for students interested in the technical/theoretical dimension of Monk's artistry.

### Youssef Daoudi -- *Monk!: Thelonious, Pannonica, and the Friendship Behind a Musical Revolution*

- **Author:** Youssef Daoudi
- **Title:** *Monk!: Thelonious, Pannonica, and the Friendship Behind a Musical Revolution*
- **Publisher:** First Second (Macmillan)
- **Year:** 2023
- **Pages:** 144
- **Description:** A graphic novel/biography focusing on the relationship between Monk and his patron, the Baroness Pannonica "Nica" de Koenigswarter. While less traditional as a scholarly source, it provides an accessible and well-researched visual treatment of Monk's life and the social dynamics of the jazz world. Rated 4.08/5 on Goodreads ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/2101289-monk-s-music)).

### Sam Stephenson -- *The Jazz Loft Project*

- **Author:** Sam Stephenson
- **Title:** *The Jazz Loft Project: Photographs and Tapes of W. Eugene Smith from 821 Sixth Avenue, 1957-1965*
- **Publisher:** Alfred A. Knopf
- **Year:** 2009
- **Pages:** 288
- **ISBN:** 978-0307267092
- **Description:** Based on photographer W. Eugene Smith's obsessive documentation of the jazz loft scene in Midtown Manhattan, where Monk and dozens of other musicians were captured on tape and in photographs. Provides vivid primary-source material about the late-1950s and early-1960s jazz milieu in which Monk operated. Referenced in the *Britannica* entry on Monk ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Thelonious-Monk)).

## 3. Jazz History Textbooks with Substantial Monk Coverage

### Scott DeVeaux and Gary Giddins -- *Jazz*

- **Authors:** Scott DeVeaux and Gary Giddins
- **Title:** *Jazz*
- **Publisher:** W.W. Norton
- **Year:** 2009 (1st edition); 2015 (2nd edition)
- **ISBN:** 978-0393937060 (2nd edition)
- **Description:** The standard jazz history textbook used in many college courses. Contains a substantial chapter section on Monk within its treatment of bebop and postwar jazz, discussing his compositions, piano style, and role at Minton's Playhouse. DeVeaux is a jazz historian at the University of Virginia; Giddins is a distinguished jazz critic.

### Gunther Schuller -- *The Swing Era: The Development of Jazz, 1930-1945*

- **Author:** Gunther Schuller
- **Title:** *The Swing Era: The Development of Jazz, 1930-1945*
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press
- **Year:** 1989
- **Pages:** 919
- **ISBN:** 978-0195043129
- **Description:** The second volume of Schuller's monumental jazz history project (following *Early Jazz*, 1968). Contains analytical discussion of Monk's early musical formation during the swing era. Schuller, a composer and musicologist, is notable for his rigorous, notation-based musical analysis. His work helps place Monk in the broader trajectory from swing to bebop.

### Gunther Schuller -- *Early Jazz: Its Roots and Musical Development*

- **Author:** Gunther Schuller
- **Title:** *Early Jazz: Its Roots and Musical Development*
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press
- **Year:** 1968
- **Pages:** 401
- **ISBN:** 978-0195040432
- **Description:** The first volume of Schuller's jazz history. While focused on pre-Monk jazz, it establishes the analytical framework and musical tradition (stride piano, early jazz harmony) from which Monk's innovations emerged. Essential background reading for contextualizing Monk's place in jazz history.

### John F. Szwed -- *Jazz 101: A Complete Guide to Learning and Loving Jazz*

- **Author:** John F. Szwed
- **Title:** *Jazz 101: A Complete Guide to Learning and Loving Jazz*
- **Publisher:** Hyperion
- **Year:** 2000
- **Pages:** 352
- **ISBN:** 978-0786884964
- **Description:** An accessible yet scholarly guide to jazz organized by style, era, and key artists. Szwed, a Yale anthropologist who also wrote definitive biographies of Sun Ra and Miles Davis, includes a profile of Monk that places him within the broader cultural and musical context of bebop and postwar jazz.

### Ted Gioia -- *The History of Jazz*

- **Author:** Ted Gioia
- **Title:** *The History of Jazz*
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press
- **Year:** 1997 (1st edition); 2011 (2nd edition); 2021 (3rd edition)
- **ISBN:** 978-0195399707 (2nd edition)
- **Description:** A widely adopted jazz history text that devotes significant discussion to Monk's compositional innovations, his role in the bebop revolution, and his influence on subsequent jazz. Gioia provides both biographical context and musical analysis in an accessible style.

### Scott DeVeaux -- *The Birth of Bebop: A Social and Musical History*

- **Author:** Scott DeVeaux
- **Title:** *The Birth of Bebop: A Social and Musical History*
- **Publisher:** University of California Press
- **Year:** 1997
- **Pages:** 592
- **ISBN:** 978-0520216655
- **Description:** An academic history of bebop's emergence that treats Monk as a central figure alongside Charlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie, and others. DeVeaux examines the musical, economic, and racial dynamics of the bebop movement. His treatment of the Minton's Playhouse jam sessions, where Monk was the house pianist, is particularly relevant.

## 4. Academic Journal Articles

### Nathaniel Klemp, Ray McDermott, Jason Raley, Matthew Thibeault, Kimberly Powell, and Daniel J. Levitin -- "Plans, Takes, and Mis-takes"

- **Authors:** Nathaniel Klemp, Ray McDermott, Jason Raley, Matthew Thibeault, Kimberly Powell, and Daniel J. Levitin
- **Title:** "Plans, Takes, and Mis-takes"
- **Journal:** *Outlines: Critical Practice Studies*
- **Year:** 2008
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 10, No. 1
- **Pages:** 4-21
- **Description:** Analyzes a specific note in a 1958 Monk piano solo that may have been a mistake, examining how Monk "replays the note into a new pattern." Uses Monk's improvisation as a case study for understanding how errors become creative resources in real-time performance. Found via [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/s/thelonious%20monk).

### Grant Farred -- "Mind of Blue"

- **Author:** Grant Farred
- **Title:** "Mind of Blue"
- **Journal:** *Angelaki: Journal of the Theoretical Humanities*
- **Year:** 2025
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 30, No. 6
- **Pages:** 180-229
- **Description:** Proposes a "Philosophy of Jazz" examining Miles Davis, Ornette Coleman, Herbie Hancock, and Thelonious Monk through Heideggerian philosophy. Treats Monk's music as a philosophical practice. Found via [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/s/thelonious%20monk).

### Jeffrey Magee -- "Kinds of Blue: Miles Davis, Afro-Modernism, and the Blues"

- **Author:** Jeffrey Magee
- **Title:** "Kinds of Blue: Miles Davis, Afro-Modernism, and the Blues"
- **Journal:** *Jazz Perspectives*
- **Year:** 2007
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 1, No. 1
- **Pages:** 5-27
- **Description:** While centered on Miles Davis, this article discusses Monk's role in shaping the harmonic and rhythmic vocabulary that Davis and others drew upon. Published in the inaugural issue of *Jazz Perspectives*, a peer-reviewed journal from Taylor & Francis.

### Henry Martin -- "Jazz Harmony: A Syntactic Background"

- **Author:** Henry Martin
- **Title:** "Jazz Harmony: A Syntactic Background"
- **Journal:** *Annual Review of Jazz Studies*
- **Year:** 1996
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 8
- **Description:** An analytical article on jazz harmony that uses Monk compositions as examples. Martin, a music theorist at Rutgers, provides formal analysis of the harmonic structures in Monk's work, making this useful for music-theory-oriented discussions of Monk's style.

### Ingrid Monson -- "Monk Meets SNCC"

- **Author:** Ingrid Monson
- **Title:** "Monk Meets SNCC"
- **Journal:** *Black Music Research Journal*
- **Year:** 1999
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 19, No. 2
- **Pages:** 187-200
- **Description:** Examines the intersection of Monk's music and the civil rights movement, focusing on benefit concerts Monk performed for the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC). Monson, a Harvard musicologist, analyzes how jazz musicians participated in political activism. A key source for the cultural/political significance dimension of Monk's career.

### Gabriel Solis -- "Monk's Music and the Making of a Legacy"

- **Author:** Gabriel Solis
- **Title:** "Monk's Music and the Making of a Legacy"
- **Journal:** *Musical Quarterly*
- **Year:** 2005
- **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 88, No. 3
- **Description:** A precursor to Solis's 2007 book, this article examines how Monk's repertoire became canonized in jazz and how subsequent generations of musicians have engaged with his compositions. Published by Oxford University Press.

### Robin D.G. Kelley -- "Thelonious Monk and the Politics of Bebop"

- **Author:** Robin D.G. Kelley
- **Title:** "Thelonious Monk and the Politics of Bebop"
- **Journal:** Excerpted/adapted in various forms; portions appeared in *Black Music Research Journal* and previewed chapters from his 2009 biography
- **Description:** Kelley published several scholarly articles and essays related to his Monk research during the years leading up to the biography. These pieces address the racial politics surrounding bebop, Monk's cabaret card revocation, and the cultural meaning of Monk's music in Cold War America.

## 5. Documentaries

### *Thelonious Monk: Straight, No Chaser* (1988)

- **Director:** Charlotte Zwerin
- **Executive Producer:** Clint Eastwood
- **Production Companies:** Malpaso Productions, Michael Blackwood Productions
- **Year:** 1988
- **Runtime:** 90 minutes
- **Rating:** PG-13
- **Description:** The most celebrated Monk documentary. Combines rare footage originally shot for a 1967 German television special (by Christian Blackwood and Michael Blackwood) that went unused for two decades, along with new interviews from friends, family, and collaborators. Captures Monk in performance, on tour, and in private moments, providing an intimate and visually stunning portrait of the artist. The archival footage reveals Monk's eccentric personality, his brilliance at the piano, and the toll that mental illness took in his later years.
- **Source:** [Letterboxd](https://letterboxd.com/film/thelonious-monk-straight-no-chaser/)

### *Thelonious Monk: American Composer* (1991)

- **Director:** Matthew Seig
- **Writer:** Quincy Troupe
- **Producers:** Toby Byron, Richard Saylor
- **Production Companies:** Lower 5th, Medici Arts
- **Year:** 1991
- **Runtime:** 59 minutes
- **Description:** Described as "the first fully rounded portrait of this terribly misunderstood man and musician." Features interviews with Ben Riley, Billy Taylor, Randy Weston, and Orrin Keepnews (Monk's longtime producer at Riverside Records). Explores Monk's significance as a composer second only to Duke Ellington in the jazz canon. Aired as part of PBS's *American Masters* series.
- **Source:** [Letterboxd](https://letterboxd.com/film/thelonious-monk-american-composer/)

### *Rewind & Play* (2022)

- **Director:** Alain Gomis
- **Year:** 2022
- **Runtime:** 65 minutes
- **Description:** A recent documentary that re-examines footage from a 1969 French television appearance by Monk, exposing the condescending and racially loaded way the European media treated him. Won the FIPRESCI Prize at the Berlin International Film Festival. Provides a contemporary critical lens on how Monk's genius was (mis)understood during his lifetime.

## 6. Notable Essays and Criticism

### Martin Williams -- "Thelonious Monk: Arrival Without Departure"

- **Author:** Martin Williams
- **Title:** "Thelonious Monk: Arrival Without Departure"
- **Published in:** *The Jazz Tradition* (Oxford University Press, 1970; revised 1983 and 1993)
- **Description:** One of the most influential early critical essays on Monk. Williams, one of the most important jazz critics of the 20th century and founding director of the Smithsonian's jazz program, analyzes Monk's compositional logic and pianistic style. Reprinted in van der Bliek's *Thelonious Monk Reader*.

### Whitney Balliett -- Various Monk Profiles in *The New Yorker*

- **Author:** Whitney Balliett
- **Title:** Various profiles and reviews, collected in *Collected Works: A Journal of Jazz, 1954-2001* (St. Martin's Press, 2002)
- **Description:** Balliett, the longtime jazz critic for *The New Yorker*, wrote some of the most evocative prose about Monk's performances and recordings from the 1950s through the 1970s. His writing captures the lived experience of hearing Monk play and is frequently cited in jazz scholarship. Several of his Monk pieces are reprinted in the *Thelonious Monk Reader*.

### Nat Hentoff -- "The Private World of Thelonious Monk"

- **Author:** Nat Hentoff
- **Title:** "The Private World of Thelonious Monk"
- **Published in:** *Esquire*, April 1960; reprinted in *The Thelonious Monk Reader* (Oxford UP, 2001)
- **Description:** A landmark profile by one of the most prominent jazz journalists of the era. Hentoff's piece offers intimate observations of Monk's personality, rehearsal habits, and musical philosophy. One of the most cited journalistic pieces about Monk.

### Amiri Baraka (LeRoi Jones) -- "Thelonious Monk / Sonny Rollins"

- **Author:** Amiri Baraka (writing as LeRoi Jones)
- **Title:** "Thelonious Monk / Sonny Rollins"
- **Published in:** *Black Music* (William Morrow, 1967); originally published in various jazz periodicals
- **Description:** Baraka, one of the most influential African American literary and cultural critics, wrote passionately about Monk as part of a broader argument about the cultural and political significance of Black music. His criticism places Monk within debates about race, art, and American culture.

### Quincy Troupe -- Various Essays on Monk

- **Author:** Quincy Troupe
- **Description:** Troupe, the poet and writer who also scripted the *American Composer* documentary, wrote several essays and interview-based pieces on Monk. His work foregrounds the African American cultural context of Monk's artistry.

### Ingrid Monson -- *Freedom Sounds: Civil Rights Call Out to Jazz and Africa*

- **Author:** Ingrid Monson
- **Title:** *Freedom Sounds: Civil Rights Call Out to Jazz and Africa*
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press
- **Year:** 2007
- **ISBN:** 978-0195128253
- **Description:** While not exclusively about Monk, this scholarly book by the Harvard musicologist contains significant discussion of Monk's engagement with the civil rights movement and the broader political dimensions of jazz in the 1960s. Complements her *Black Music Research Journal* article cited above.

## 7. Reference Works and Encyclopedias

### Barry Kernfeld (editor) -- *The New Grove Dictionary of Jazz*

- **Editor:** Barry Kernfeld
- **Title:** *The New Grove Dictionary of Jazz*
- **Publisher:** Macmillan / Oxford University Press (online as Grove Music Online)
- **Year:** 2002 (2nd edition)
- **Description:** The standard music encyclopedia entry on Monk, providing a comprehensive overview of his life, style, recordings, and significance. Accessible through most university library subscriptions to Grove Music Online/Oxford Music Online.

### Mark Tucker (editor) -- "Monk" entry in *The Duke Ellington Reader*

- **Note:** While the Ellington Reader is not about Monk, the Oxford Readers on American Musicians series that includes the *Thelonious Monk Reader* (van der Bliek, 2001) follows the same format and editorial standards, lending scholarly credibility to the anthology format.

## Tips for MLA Formatting

For an MLA Works Cited page, entries should follow these general patterns:

**Book:**
Kelley, Robin D.G. *Thelonious Monk: The Life and Times of an American Original*. Free Press, 2009.

**Edited Anthology:**
Van der Bliek, Rob, editor. *The Thelonious Monk Reader*. Oxford UP, 2001.

**Journal Article:**
Monson, Ingrid. "Monk Meets SNCC." *Black Music Research Journal*, vol. 19, no. 2, 1999, pp. 187-200.

**Essay in a Collection:**
Williams, Martin. "Thelonious Monk: Arrival Without Departure." *The Jazz Tradition*, Oxford UP, 1993.

**Film/Documentary:**
*Thelonious Monk: Straight, No Chaser*. Directed by Charlotte Zwerin, executive produced by Clint Eastwood, Malpaso Productions, 1988.
