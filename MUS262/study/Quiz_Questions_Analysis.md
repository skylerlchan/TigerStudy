### Question 3: Era/Style Comparison
**Q:** Name two ways in which the big bands of the 1930s and 1940s differed from the New Orleans bands of the 1910s and 1920s.

**Pattern:** Compares two different time periods/styles covered in the course; requires understanding of evolution

### Question 4: Artist Comparison
**Q:** How do jazz vocalists Billie Holiday and Ella Fitzgerald differ in their approach to singing jazz?

**Pattern:** Compares two artists with different approaches/styles from the same era

---

### Question 5: Defining Characteristics of a Movement

**Pattern:** Asks for key features that define a specific movement/style

## PREDICTED QUIZ 2 QUESTIONS (Weeks 5-7: Hard Bop, Modal Jazz, Free Jazz)

### Predicted Question 1: Musical Influences/Characteristics
**Possible Q:**
- "What is one characteristic of gospel music that influenced hard bop? What is one characteristic of impressionist classical music that influenced modal jazz?"
- OR: "How did gospel and R&B influence hard bop in the 1950s?"
- OR: "What role did Eastern/Indian music play in the development of modal jazz?"

**Why:** Follows Quiz 1's pattern of asking about foundational influences

**Answer approach:**
- Gospel → syncopation, call-and-response, soulful feel, church harmonies (Hard Bop)
- Impressionism → colorful harmonies, pedal tones, spacious textures (Modal Jazz)
- Eastern music → drones, repetition, meditative quality, spiritual seeking (Modal Jazz)

---

### Predicted Question 2: Specific Concept from Readings
**Possible Q:**
- "What does Ingrid Monson mean by 'saying something' in jazz, as described in *Saying Something*, Chapter 3?"
- OR: "What does Paul Berliner mean by 'thinking in jazz' in his book *Thinking in Jazz*?"
- OR: "According to Szwed, what is 'soul jazz' and how does it relate to hard bop?"

**Why:** Follows Quiz 1's pattern of citing specific reading concepts (like Schuller's "Ellington effect")

**Answer approach:**
- **Monson's "Saying Something":** Musicians communicate meaning through playing - emotional depth, cultural references, conversational quality in improvisation
- **Berliner's "Thinking in Jazz":** Musicians develop internalized vocabulary of patterns/phrases that become second nature; mental library for spontaneous improvisation
- **Szwed's "Soul Jazz":** Substyle of hard bop emphasizing funky, gospel-influenced grooves; tied to Black church and R&B

---

### Predicted Question 3: Era/Style Comparison (MOST LIKELY - ~30% emphasis)
**Possible Q:**
- "Name two ways in which modal jazz (Week 6) differs from hard bop (Week 5)"
- OR: "What are two musical differences between modal jazz and free jazz?"
- OR: "How does free jazz differ from hard bop? (Two ways)"

**Why:** This is CRITICAL - the study guide emphasizes ~30% of quiz will be three-week comparisons

**Answer approaches:**

**Hard Bop vs Modal Jazz:**
1. **Harmony:** Hard Bop uses complex, rapid chord changes; Modal Jazz uses minimal harmony (one or two modes per section)
2. **Rhythm/Feel:** Hard Bop emphasizes strong, locked-in groove; Modal Jazz has more spacious, floating feel

**Modal Jazz vs Free Jazz:**
1. **Harmony:** Modal Jazz uses modes/scales as structure; Free Jazz has no predetermined harmony (atonal)
2. **Form:** Modal Jazz still has structure (modes, pulse); Free Jazz abandons all predetermined forms

**Hard Bop vs Free Jazz:**
1. **Rhythm:** Hard Bop has strong, steady groove; Free Jazz often has no steady pulse (free time)
2. **Structure:** Hard Bop uses traditional forms (AABA, blues); Free Jazz abandons all fixed forms

### Predicted Question 4: Artist Comparison
**Possible Q:**
- "How do tenor saxophonists Sonny Rollins and John Coltrane differ in their approach?"
- OR: "Compare Miles Davis's approach in 'Oleo' (hard bop) vs. 'So What' (modal jazz)"
- OR: "How does Horace Silver's piano style differ from Bill Evans' impressionistic approach?"
- OR: "How does Ornette Coleman's free jazz approach differ from Charlie Parker's bebop?"

**Why:** Follows Quiz 1's pattern of comparing two artists with different styles

**Answer approaches:**

**Rollins vs Coltrane:**
1. **Rollins:** Playful, rhythmically inventive, uses calypso/Caribbean influences; focuses on melodic development
2. **Coltrane:** More spiritually intense, harmonically complex; used "sheets of sound" and modal exploration

**Miles "Oleo" vs "So What":**
1. **"Oleo":** Fast bebop tempo with rapid chord changes; virtuosic navigation of harmony
2. **"So What":** Slow, spacious modal approach with just two modes; melodic development over technical display

**Silver vs Evans:**
1. **Silver:** Funky, gospel-influenced, accessible; founded soul jazz
2. **Evans:** Impressionistic, classical-influenced, delicate; chamber jazz approach

**Coleman vs Parker:**
1. **Coleman:** Abandoned chord changes entirely; raw, vocal-like plastic alto sax sound
2. **Parker:** Master of navigating complex harmony; pure, controlled bebop tone

---

### Predicted Question 5: Defining Characteristics of a Movement
**Possible Q:**
- "Name two defining characteristics of hard bop"
- OR: "Name two defining characteristics of modal jazz"
- OR: "Name two defining characteristics of free jazz"

**Why:** Directly parallels Quiz 1's bebop question

**Answer approaches:**

**Hard Bop:**
1. **Gospel/blues influence:** Incorporated church music, R&B, and blues into bebop's foundation; funkier, more soulful
2. **Strong rhythm section:** Emphasized groove and swing; drummers like Art Blakey drove the band with powerful, interactive playing

**Modal Jazz:**
1. **Scale-based improvisation:** Used modes (scales) instead of chord progressions as harmonic foundation
2. **Spaciousness:** Slower tempos, less harmonic density, more room for melodic exploration and contemplation

**Free Jazz:**
1. **No predetermined harmony:** Abandoned chord progressions and tonal centers entirely; completely free improvisation
2. **Collective improvisation:** All musicians improvised simultaneously without soloist/accompanist roles; no fixed forms or structures

## QUICK REFERENCE: WEEK IDENTIFICATION

### If you hear HARD BOP (Week 5):
- Strong, steady groove (tap your foot easily)
- Complex chord changes but soulful feel
- Blues/gospel influences
- Funky, earthy quality
- Walking bass, driving drums
- Examples: "Blues March," "Song for My Father," "St. Thomas"

### If you hear MODAL JAZZ (Week 6):
- Spacious, floating feeling
- Minimal chord changes
- One or two modes for extended periods
- Contemplative, meditative quality
- Quiet, intimate often
- Examples: "So What," "My Favorite Things," "Maiden Voyage"

### If you hear FREE JAZZ (Week 7):
- No steady pulse or beat
- Atonal (no clear key)
- Chaotic, intense, confrontational
- Angular melodies, extreme sounds
- Collective improvisation
- Examples: "Lonely Woman," "Ghosts," "Expression"
