## Summary

### Free Jazz Movement & Historical Context

- Free jazz emerged in 1960s as extension of bebop/hard bop
- Movement coincided with civil rights era - “freedom” became rallying cry
- Musicians moved away from traditional chord structures toward musical freedom
- Represented both musical and social liberation for Black Americans

### Ornette Coleman - Pioneer of Free Jazz

- Born 1930 Texas, died 2015 at age 85
- Self-taught saxophonist, not focused on traditional virtuoso technique
- Played white plastic saxophone early in career (couldn’t afford brass)
- Gave distinctive sound to early recordings (1958-1959)- Charlie Parker also used plastic sax on “Jazz at Massey Hall” (1953)
- Violent audience reactions to his radical style
- 1950 New Orleans: dragged from club, beaten, horn broken
- Debut recording “Something Else” (1958) on Contemporary Records
- Introduced concept of “harmolodics” (1972) - never fully explained the theory
- Spontaneous harmony through melodic conversation- No predetermined chord structures

### Key Recordings & Musical Evolution

- “The Shape of Jazz to Come” (1959) - most famous album
- Featured “Lonely Woman” - his most famous composition- Recorded without piano to avoid harmonic constraints- Jackson Pollock painting on album cover
- “Free Jazz” (1961) - gave movement its name
- 40-minute collective improvisation- Double quartet format- Extremely challenging listening experience
- Collaboration with John Coltrane
- “The Avant-Garde” (1960) with Coltrane playing Ornette’s compositions- Coltrane influenced by Coleman’s approach, evolved toward free jazz- Coltrane’s later works like “Ascension” (1965) pushed boundaries further- Lost audience with radical evolution from “A Love Supreme”
