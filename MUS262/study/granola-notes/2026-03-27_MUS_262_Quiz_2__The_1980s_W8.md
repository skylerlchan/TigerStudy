## Summary

### Neoclassical Jazz Approach

- Conservative movement emphasizing fidelity to specific canon and traditional practices
- Jazz must swing in certain way with harmonies conforming to established values
- Boundaries defined by key artists rather than generic style communities

### Branford Marsalis Analysis

- Older brother of Wynton, more open-minded with youthful spirit
- Influenced by Wayne Shorter, Sonny Rollins concepts
- “Footprints” comparison (1964 original vs 1987 “Random Abstract”):
- Similar melodic ideas to Shorter’s version- More hard bebop execution style- Increased rhythm section interaction and banter
- Career diversity beyond traditional jazz:
- Two hip-hop albums in 90s (pseudonym “Buckshot LeFonque”)- Two classical albums (1986 “Romances for Saxophone”, 2001 “Creation”)- Tonight Show band leader (early 90s) - left due to commercial constraints
- Credited for modernizing soprano saxophone alongside Coltrane

### Steve Coleman’s Innovation

- Born 1956 Chicago, influenced by Charlie Parker and Maceo Parker (James Brown’s saxophonist)
- Developed “symmetry” improvisation concept based on mathematics
- Founded M-Base Collective (1985): “Macro Basic Array of Structured Extemporization”
- Fused jazz with funk and hip-hop elements- Collective approach to new musical concepts
- “Motherland Pulse” debut album featured straight-ahead acoustic jazz
- Unique alto saxophone voice developed from teenage experience with bee near ear

### Michael Brecker Legacy

- Philadelphia-born (1949-2007), died at 57 from bone marrow cancer
- Multi-Grammy winner, most prolific session saxophonist of his era
- Left Indiana University at 21 to pursue full-time music career
- Influenced by John Coltrane but developed distinct virtuosic style
- “Syzygy” (1987) showcased intense improvisational approach
- Title means opposition of sun and moon- Demonstrated rock-influenced jazz language on saxophone
