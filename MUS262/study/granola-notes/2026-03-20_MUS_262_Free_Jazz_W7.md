# MUS 262: Free Jazz (W7)

**Date:** 2026-03-20


## Summary

### Albert Ayler - Tenor Saxophone Pioneer

- Unique artistic register style with screaming upper register
- Influenced by Coleman Hawkins’ “Body and Soul” approach- Pushed saxophone beyond normal range with extreme emotional content
- Moved to Sweden (1962), joined important ensemble (1963)
- Tragic personal story
- Brother Donald (trumpet player) had nervous breakdown (1967)- Albert deeply affected by brother’s mental health struggles- Committed suicide by jumping into East River (1974)
- Major influence on Coltrane, Pharoah Sanders, Archie Shepp
- Most famous recording: “Ghosts” from “Spiritual Unity” album
- Recorded July 10, 1964- Featured Gary Peacock (bass), Sunny Murray (drums)- Connected to “Fire Music” term coined by Archie Shepp (1965)

### Cecil Taylor - Classical Training Meets Free Jazz

- Born 1929 NYC, died 2018 at 89
- New England Conservatory classical piano training
- Controversial dense, aggressive playing style
- Critics said he “doesn’t swing” (similar to early Monk criticism)- Perpetual outsider throughout career
- Miles Davis encounter: walked past Cecil without acknowledgment
- Early collaboration with Coltrane on “Coltrane Time” (1958)
- Recording session tensions with conservative bebop musicians- Kenny Dorham, Chuck Israels, Louis Hayes all disliked Cecil’s approach- Coltrane initially said he loved all musicians “except Cecil Taylor”- By 1966, Coltrane praised Cecil’s work
- “Unit Structures” album (1966) - most famous work
- “Tales (8 Whisps)” demonstrates compositional and piano skills- Classical music influence evident in composition approach- Dynamic range usage (soft to loud transitions)- Gestural playing style with burst notes

### Eric Dolphy - Multi-Instrumentalist Virtuoso

- Born 1928 Los Angeles, virtuoso on alto sax, flute, bass clarinet
- Introduced bass clarinet to jazz
- Career progression: Mingus band (1959-1961) → Coltrane band (1961-1962)
- 1961 DownBeat magazine called his music “anti-jazz” due to angry sound
- Coltrane’s response: “I’m angry at myself because I can’t play everything I want”
- Practiced outside mimicking bird sounds for unique aesthetic
- Clean lifestyle: no drugs/alcohol, suffered from diabetes
- Tragic death (1964, age 36)
- Collapsed on stage in Copenhagen during diabetic coma- Hospital staff assumed he was drug user, provided inadequate care
- “Out to Lunch” (1964) - final album featuring all original compositions
- “Straight Up and Down” demonstrates visual-musical connection- Featured Freddie Hubbard, Bobby Hutcherson, Richard Davis, Tony Williams
