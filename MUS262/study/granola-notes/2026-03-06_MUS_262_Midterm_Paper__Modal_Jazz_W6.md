### Modal Jazz Artists Analysis

- **Wayne Shorter**
- Born 1933 New Jersey, died recently in late 80s- Key composer/saxophonist in jazz history- Started with Art Blakey’s Jazz Messengers (1960)- Initially sounded like young Coltrane- Resident composer for Miles Davis 1960s quintet- Co-founded Weather Report fusion group 1970s- “Speak No Evil” (1964) - title track analysis
- Features Freddy Hubbard (trumpet), Herbie Hancock (piano), Ron Carter (bass), Elvin Jones (drums)- Shorter favored single note lines vs. Coltrane’s scalar runs- More rhythmic elements, dramatic pauses
- **John Coltrane comparison**
- “A Love Supreme” recorded December 24, 1964 (2 weeks after Speak No Evil)- Coltrane’s spiritual awakening 1957, album as “musical praise to the Almighty”- Acknowledgment movement features overdubbed chanting (Coltrane recorded voice twice)- Harder, thicker tenor sax tone vs. earlier Miles Davis recordings- Last modal jazz record before moving to free jazz territory- Multiple versions exist with Archie Shepp, Art Davis

### Cool Jazz & Bossa Nova

- **Dave Brubeck**
- Born 1920 San Francisco, died 2012 age 91- West Coast cool jazz scene leader- 1954 Time Magazine controversy - first jazz cover story featuring white musician over Black pioneers- “Take Five” from “Time Out” (1959)
